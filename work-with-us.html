Work with us

Display advertising, sponsored recipes, newsletter sponsorship, photography and licensed recipe development for food, grocery and kitchenware brands in Cyprus, Greece and the wider EU. Everything we publish for a client is labelled as paid, at the top of the page.

Who reads Meraki Table

Home cooks in Cyprus first, then Cypriots and Greek speakers living abroad, then a steady English-language readership across the EU and the United Kingdom cooking Eastern Mediterranean food. Most are shopping weekly for a household of two to five, cooking on weeknights rather than for occasions, and they are the kind of reader who weighs flour, buys what is cheap at the laiki and will change a menu because the artichokes looked good.

What we offer

Display advertising
Standard IAB units placed below the main content, clearly labelled, never overlaying a recipe, a button or the navigation. No interstitials, no pop-unders, no video that plays sound by itself. Ad density is deliberately low, and consent for advertising cookies is collected before any of it runs.
Sponsored recipes
An original recipe developed, cooked, photographed and written by us, using your product where it genuinely belongs in a Cypriot kitchen. Labelled as sponsored at the very top of the page, above the recipe.
Newsletter sponsorship
One clearly labelled placement in the Thursday email. One sponsor per send, never more. Subscriber addresses are never shared, sold or exported to you.
Recipe development
Recipes developed for your own channels, packaging or in-store leaflets, tested to the same standard as everything here. Licensed to you, and not published on this site unless we agree otherwise. Available in English, and in Greek on request.
Photography
Food photography in daylight for your own campaigns, shot in the same style as the site. Half or full days in Limassol, or anywhere on the island for travel at cost.

How we handle disclosure

EU consumer law, applied in Cyprus through the Consumer Protection Law and enforced by the Consumer Protection Service of the Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, prohibits misleading commercial practices. Paid content that a reader has to work out for themselves is one of them. Our approach goes past the minimum, because it is better for everyone, including you.

  • Sponsored recipes carry a label at the top of the page, above the headline and above the recipe, in a bordered box. Not in the footer, not in a hashtag, not in fine print at the end.
  • Affiliate links are disclosed at the link itself, in a short line directly beside or beneath it, as well as in the article and in our site-wide disclosure.
  • Newsletter sponsorship is labelled inside the email, above the placement.
  • Gifted products are declared in the article if we write about them, whether or not money changed hands.
  • Advertising units are visually distinct from editorial and carry the word Advertisement.
  • Reader data stays with us. We process personal data under the GDPR (EU) 2016/679 and Cyprus Law 125(I)/2018, and a sponsor never receives it.

What we will not do

  • Publish a recipe we have not cooked and tested at least twice.
  • Accept payment in exchange for a positive opinion or a particular rating.
  • Make any health, medical, nutritional, weight-loss or therapeutic claim about a product. EU Regulation 1924/2006 restricts those claims to an authorised list, and we do not go near them at any price.
  • Publish comparative claims we cannot substantiate, or superlatives such as best or number one.
  • Run content that is not obviously identifiable as advertising.
  • Share subscriber email addresses or reader data with a sponsor.
  • Promote alcohol to a general audience, gambling, weight-loss products, supplements or infant formula. Cypriot wine, commandaria and zivania may appear inside a recipe as an ingredient, which is not the same thing as a drinks campaign.

If a brief needs any of the above we will say no in the first email rather than waste your budget. We are equally happy to be told no by you, and we never write publicly about a partnership that did not go ahead.

How a sponsored recipe works

  1. Brief

    You tell us the product, the audience and the window. We tell you honestly whether it fits a Cypriot home kitchen, and say so if it does not.

  2. Concept

    We propose two or three recipe ideas, usually tied to what is in season here that month. You choose one. You do not write the method.

  3. Cook and shoot

    We cook it at least three times, then photograph it in daylight in the same style as the rest of the site.

  4. Review

    You see the draft and can correct anything factual about your product. Editorial opinion stays ours.

  5. Publish

    The page goes live with the sponsorship label at the top and stays up permanently unless you ask otherwise.

An example of the labelling in practice is on our roast pumpkin, chickpea and rocket salad.

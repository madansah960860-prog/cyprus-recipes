BBQ & Outdoor

Cooking outside here means charcoal. A foukou in the yard, a kettle on the balcony, a grate over blockwork at a village house. Everything below is written for coals first, with notes for a hooded gas barbecue where the method has to change.

Charcoal, and why nobody here is in a hurry

The Cypriot outdoor cook is not making dinner. They are making an afternoon. Souvla goes on the foukou at midday for a lunch that will still be going at five, and the fire is the reason everybody stays in the yard rather than drifting inside. If you treat the coals as a timer you have misunderstood the whole event.

The foukou is the standard piece of kit: a long narrow charcoal trough, usually stainless, with a motorised spit across the top. Big pieces of pork shoulder or lamb go on the skewer seasoned with salt, pepper and sometimes dried oregano, the coals are banked low, and the motor turns it for an hour and a half or two while the cook sits down. Thin, fast things go on the separate grate: sheftalia, halloumi, loukanika, whole spring onions, bread.

Charcoal, not gas, and the difference is not snobbery. Gas is clean, fast and perfectly good for a Tuesday. It cannot give you the smoke, or the slow render of fat down the length of a skewer, or the smell that tells the neighbours what day it is. Buy hardwood lump charcoal rather than briquettes if you can, light it with a chimney starter rather than firelighter fluid, and wait until the coals have gone grey before anything touches the grate.

Two zones still matter, whatever you are burning. Bank the coals deep on one side and thin on the other, or on a gas barbecue turn one side to high and leave the other off, and move food between colour and finishing instead of standing over it hoping. A lid, if you have one, turns the whole thing into an oven, which is what chicken pieces, whole fish and pizza need.

Clean while it is hot. Scrape the grate with a folded ball of foil or a wire brush straight after the food comes off, while the fat is still liquid. A cold grate is miserable work, which is why so many of them never get done.

On fire safety, Cyprus takes this seriously and so should you. From spring through to late autumn the island is tinder, and there are periods when lighting a fire outdoors is restricted or banned outright. Keep the fire well away from dry grass, pine needles, the house and anything overhanging, never leave it lit and unattended, have water within reach, and drown the coals properly rather than leaving them to smoulder. Check the Department of Forests notices before you light anything near scrub or forest, and call 1407 for a forest fire or 112 for the emergency services.

What a long Sunday actually looks like

In a village taverna the meat is the last thing to arrive and nobody minds. Bread, olives, pickled cauliflower and a bowl of tahini land first, then the cold plates, then halloumi and loukanika off the grate while the souvla is still turning, then salad, then eventually the meat itself carved straight off the skewer onto a board in the middle of the table. Potatoes go in the coals. Nobody gets a plate of their own arranged by anyone.

Copy the order at home and the cooking gets easier, because it spreads the work across four hours instead of compressing it into twenty minutes. Put out something to eat the moment the first guest arrives, cook the small things as people talk, and let the big piece of meat take as long as it takes.

Quantities for a long lunch

The most common mistake is not the cooking, it is the shopping. Everybody buys three kinds of meat and one salad, and then half the meat goes in the fridge. Numbers that hold up for eight adults eating over an afternoon:

  • 1.6 kgpork shoulder for souvla, off the bone
  • 16sheftalia, two a head
  • 500 ghalloumi, sliced 1 cm thick
  • 3 kghardwood charcoal for a two hour fire

Add two vegetable dishes, one of them raw, a kilo of potatoes for the coals, two loaves of village bread and a bowl of lemon wedges. That is generous without being ridiculous, and it assumes at least two people will arrive holding something.

Sheftalia, and why they split

Sheftalia are minced pork and lamb with onion, parsley and plenty of black pepper, wrapped in caul fat rather than a skin. The caul melts as it cooks and bastes the meat, which is the entire trick. They split for two reasons: the parcels were rolled too loosely, or the fire was too hot and the fat rendered before the meat set. Roll them tight, start them over the cooler side of the coals for five minutes, then move them across for colour. Ask a Cypriot butcher for bolia and they will know exactly what you want.

Barbecued Corn with Lime and Smoked Paprika Butter

Husks off, straight onto the grate over the edge of the coals, and the butter goes on afterwards rather than before so it does not flare up. The flavoured butter can be rolled and frozen weeks ahead.

Method

  1. Make the butter

    Mash the softened butter with the smoked paprika, lime zest, garlic, salt flakes and coriander. Roll it into a log in baking paper and refrigerate for 15 minutes, or freeze for later.

  2. Heat the barbecue

    Get the grate to medium-high, which on charcoal means coals gone grey and banked a little thinner. Corn burns quickly over a roaring fire and stays raw inside.

  3. Oil the corn, not the bars

    Rub the cobs lightly with neutral oil. Oiling the corn rather than the grate means less smoke and fewer flare-ups.

  4. Turn every two minutes

    Grill for 8 to 10 minutes, turning a quarter turn every 2 minutes, until each side has dark blistered patches and the kernels are bright yellow.

  5. Butter off the heat

    Move the corn to a platter and rub slices of the cold butter over the hot cobs so it melts into the kernels. Serve with lime wedges.

Cook's tips

  • Buy corn in its husk. Pre-husked corn on a foam tray dries out. Peel back a strip and check the kernels are plump and pale gold, not shrunken. Cypriot corn is at its best from July through September, and the laiki has it before the supermarkets do.
  • Do not salt the corn before grilling. Salt draws moisture to the surface, which steams the kernels instead of charring them. It goes in the butter instead.
  • Make double butter. It keeps for a month in the freezer, sliced straight from frozen onto steak, fish or baked potatoes.

Storage and make ahead

The butter keeps 2 weeks refrigerated and 3 months frozen. Cooked corn keeps 2 days, and leftover kernels cut off the cob are excellent in fritters or salads. Do not freeze cooked corn on the cob, the texture goes watery.

Allergens. Contains dairy. Gluten free as written. Use a plant-based spread for a dairy free version. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Beetroot and Beef Burgers with Tarragon Mayo

Beetroot with garlic is already a meze plate here, so putting it in a burger is a shorter walk than it sounds. The only real technique is handling the mince as little as possible, because compressed mince makes a dense, bouncy patty.

Method

  1. Form the patties lightly

    Divide the mince into four and shape each portion into a patty about 2 cm thick and slightly wider than the bun. Handle it as little as possible. Press a shallow dimple into the centre of each so they do not dome.

  2. Season the outside only

    Season the patties on both surfaces with the salt and pepper right before they go on. Salt mixed through mince ahead of time changes the texture towards sausage.

  3. Mix the mayo

    Stir the mayonnaise, mustard, tarragon and lemon juice together and refrigerate.

  4. Drain the beetroot properly

    Lay the beetroot slices on paper towel for 10 minutes. Wet beetroot is what turns a burger bun into pink mush.

  5. Cook on the flat plate

    Heat a flat plate or a heavy pan set over the coals to high with the oil. Cook the patties for 4 minutes, flip once, add the cheese, and cook another 3 to 4 minutes. Do not press them with the spatula, that squeezes out the juices.

  6. Toast the buns and build

    Toast the cut buns on the plate for 1 minute. Spread both halves with mayo, then build: lettuce, patty with cheese, tomato, beetroot, top. The lettuce underneath protects the base from the juices.

Cook's tips

  • Ask the butcher to mince chuck for you. Supermarket premium mince is often too lean, around 5 per cent fat, and lean mince makes dry burgers. You want closer to 20 per cent, and any Cypriot butcher will do it while you wait.
  • The dimple is not optional. Patties contract as they cook and puff up in the middle. A shallow thumbprint in the raw patty keeps them flat, which means the fillings stay on.
  • Rest for 3 minutes. Straight off the plate, the juices run out into the bun. Three minutes on a warm plate makes a noticeable difference.
  • Flat plate, not the open grate. An open grate lets the fat drip into the coals and flare, which coats the meat in soot. A flat plate or heavy pan over the fire keeps the fat where it does some good.

Storage and make ahead

Raw patties can be shaped a day ahead, layered between baking paper and refrigerated, or frozen for 2 months. The mayo keeps 4 days in the fridge. Cooked patties keep 2 days and are good chopped through a salad. Assembled burgers do not keep.

Allergens. Contains gluten (bun), egg (mayonnaise), dairy (cheese) and mustard. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Barbecued Chicken Pizza with Red Onion

A covered barbecue or a kettle full of charcoal runs far hotter than a domestic oven, which is exactly what a pizza base wants. Use bought bases or dough from the bakery counter and this is a thirty minute job.

Method

  1. Heat the barbecue with the hood down

    Bring it to about 250°C with the lid down, which takes 10 to 15 minutes on gas and about 25 on charcoal once the coals are lit. Then bank the coals to the sides, or switch the middle burners off, to make an indirect zone.

  2. Cook the chicken first

    If using raw chicken, grill the thighs for 5 minutes a side over direct heat, rest, then slice. Leftover roast chicken skips this step entirely.

  3. Prepare the bases

    Dust a flat tray or pizza peel with semolina and lay the base on it. Brush the rim with olive oil. Semolina acts like ball bearings and lets the pizza slide off.

  4. Top lightly

    Spread a thin layer of passata, leaving a 2 cm border. Add mozzarella, chicken, red onion and oregano. Overloading is the main reason home pizzas go soggy in the middle.

  5. Cook over the indirect zone

    Slide the pizza onto the grate over the indirect zone, close the lid, and cook for 8 to 10 minutes until the base is crisp and the cheese is blistered. Rotate once halfway, because one side of a charcoal fire is always hotter than the other.

  6. Finish and rest

    Scatter with rocket, rest for 2 minutes, then cut. Cutting immediately drags the cheese off the base.

Cook's tips

  • Use a pizza stone or steel if you own one. Heat it with the barbecue for a full 20 minutes. It gives the base the sharp bottom heat that makes the difference between good and very good. Unglazed terracotta tiles from a builders' merchant do the same job for a few euro.
  • Slice the onion paper thin. Thick onion will still be raw and harsh in ten minutes. A mandolin or a very sharp knife, and thinner than feels necessary.
  • Do not put the rocket on before cooking. It burns to ash in under a minute at 250°C. Leaves go on at the end, always.

Storage and make ahead

Cooked chicken can be grilled a day ahead. Assembled pizza does not keep raw, the base goes soggy within 20 minutes. Leftover cooked pizza keeps 2 days and reheats best in a dry frying pan with a lid, not a microwave.

Allergens. Contains gluten and dairy. Read the packaging in your own kitchen, particularly bought bases.

Charred Vegetable Platter with Whipped Feta

Courgette, red pepper and aubergine cooked hard over the coals and dressed while still hot, so they drink up the vinegar instead of sitting in it. In August the laiki sells all three for almost nothing, and this is the dish that makes the vegetarians at a long lunch feel like guests rather than an afterthought.

Method

  1. Salt the eggplant

    Lay the aubergine rounds on a rack, salt both sides with half the salt flakes and leave for 15 minutes. Pat dry. This seasons it and firms the flesh so it absorbs less oil.

  2. Oil the vegetables

    Brush all the vegetables with olive oil and season with the remaining salt. Oil the food, not the grate.

  3. Whip the feta

    Blitz the feta, yoghurt and lemon juice in a food processor for 90 seconds until smooth and airy. If you have no processor, mash it with a fork and beat hard with a whisk.

  4. Grill in batches

    Cook over a hot grate: peppers skin side down for 8 minutes, aubergine 4 minutes a side, courgette 3 minutes a side, onion 5 minutes a side. Move each one off when it is charred and just tender.

  5. Dress while hot

    Pile the vegetables straight into a bowl and toss with the red wine vinegar and crushed garlic while still hot. They will absorb the dressing as they cool.

  6. Assemble

    Spread the whipped feta across a large platter, pile the dressed vegetables on top and scatter with torn mint. Serve at room temperature.

Cook's tips

  • Cook each vegetable separately. They finish at different times and a mixed pile means half of it is raw and half is collapsed. It takes longer but it is the whole difference.
  • Steam the peppers after grilling. Put the charred pieces in a bowl covered with a plate for 10 minutes and the blackened skin lifts off with your fingers.
  • Room temperature, not cold. Straight from the fridge the olive oil goes cloudy and the flavours flatten. Take it out 30 minutes before serving, which in a Cypriot July is about four.

Storage and make ahead

The whole platter can be made a day ahead and improves overnight. Store the vegetables and the feta separately in the fridge for up to 3 days and assemble on the day. Add the mint at the last minute. Do not freeze.

Allergens. Contains dairy. Gluten free as written. For a vegan version, replace the whipped feta with a thick hummus. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Barbecued Whole Snapper with Lemon and Oregano

Whole fish over fire is what every seaside taverna from Latchi to Protaras is doing, and it is much easier than it looks and much cheaper per kilo than fillets. The two things that matter are a very clean, very hot surface and the confidence to leave it alone.

For the fish

For the dressing

0 of 10 ticked

Method

  1. Score and season

    Pat the fish dry. Cut three diagonal slashes through the thickest part of each side, down to the bone. Rub oil and salt flakes over the skin and into the slashes.

  2. Stuff the cavity

    Fill the cavity with lemon slices, oregano sprigs and parsley. Do not overfill it, the fish needs to sit flat on the plate.

  3. Clean and heat the plate

    Scrape the flat plate or grate clean, get it properly hot, then oil it with a folded piece of paper towel held in tongs. A dirty or cool surface is the only reason fish sticks.

  4. Cook the first side

    Lay the fish down and close the lid, or cover it with a roasting tray if your fire is open. Cook for 12 minutes without touching it. If it does not release when you slide a spatula under it, give it another minute.

  5. Turn once

    Use two spatulas, one at the head end and one at the tail, and turn it in a single confident movement. Cook the second side for 10 to 12 minutes, covered again.

  6. Check and rest

    The flesh at the thickest slash should be opaque and pull away from the bone. Rest for 5 minutes, then whisk the dressing and pour it over. Serve with lemon wedges.

Cook's tips

  • The slashes are not decoration. They let heat into the thickest part so the shoulders finish at the same time as the belly, and they give you somewhere to check for doneness without wrecking the presentation.
  • Ask the fishmonger to scale and gut it. They will do it in ninety seconds. Scaling a fish in a home kitchen puts scales in places you will still be finding in a fortnight.
  • Buy what looks good, not what the recipe says. Tsipoura, lavraki, fangri and a decent-sized barbouni all work. Clear eyes, firm flesh and a clean smell matter far more than the species, and what came off a Cypriot boat this morning beats anything flown in.
  • A fish basket helps. If turning a whole fish makes you nervous, a hinged wire fish basket costs around €15 and removes the problem entirely. Superhome Center and Stephanis both carry them.

Storage and make ahead

The dressing can be made 3 days ahead. Cooked fish keeps 1 day in the fridge and is very good flaked through a salad the next day. Do not freeze cooked fish. Buy it on the day you plan to cook it, and if you are near a harbour, buy it in the morning.

Allergens. Contains fish. Gluten free and dairy free as written. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

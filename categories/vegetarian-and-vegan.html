Vegetarian & Vegan

Plant cooking that is not built around a substitute for meat, which is how Cyprus has always done it. Every recipe notes exactly what to change for a vegan version, so nobody has to guess whether the honey matters.

Where the flavour comes from

Take meat out of a dish and you lose three things: fat, salt and browning. Most disappointing vegetarian food has simply not replaced them. So the recipes here lean on roasting rather than boiling, on toasted nuts and seeds for fat and crunch, and on acid at the end to stop everything tasting the same.

Cypriot cooking solves the fat problem with olive oil and is not shy about the quantity. A plate of boiled greens with a mean drizzle is a plate of boiled greens. The same greens under a proper pour, with lemon and salt flakes, is the dish. If the amount of oil in a recipe here looks like a lot, it is deliberate, and it is the cheapest ingredient in the bowl.

The habit worth building is salting in layers. Salt the vegetables before they roast, salt the dressing, taste again at the end. A vegetable dish seasoned only at the table always tastes flat, because salt on the surface is not the same as salt cooked through. The other habit is finishing with something raw and sharp: chopped parsley, sliced onion left in lemon juice for ten minutes, a spoon of pickled cauliflower from the jar.

On labelling: we mark a recipe vegan only when every ingredient as written is plant-based. Recipes with a straightforward swap are marked as having a vegan option, and the swap is spelled out rather than left to you. Watch for honey, fish sauce, parmesan and traditional halloumi (both made with animal rennet), Worcestershire sauce, and some wines and vinegars.

Cyprus has been doing this for a very long time

Nobody on this island had to be persuaded to cook without animal products. The Orthodox fasting calendar took care of that centuries ago, and the food it produced has a name: nistisima. Depending on how strictly a household keeps it, that means no meat, no dairy, no eggs and often no fish with a backbone, for the whole of Lent, for the weeks before Christmas, for Wednesdays and Fridays through the year. Roughly half the calendar, in some houses.

What is striking is that none of it reads as deprivation. The nistisima table is pulses, wild greens, olive oil, lemon, tahini, bread, olives, pickles, potatoes, and whatever the season is giving. It is the best argument for plant cooking anyone has ever made, and it was made by people who were not trying to make it.

Louvi me lahana is the dish to start with: black-eyed beans boiled with chard or whatever greens are around, tipped into a bowl, and drowned in good olive oil and lemon at the table. That is the entire recipe. It is served warm or at room temperature, usually with a piece of fish or a lump of feta for anyone not fasting, and it is eaten more often in Cypriot homes than anything you will find on a menu.

Beyond it there is a whole pantry: fava and split peas, brown lentils with rice, chickpeas roasted or stewed, gigantes in tomato, kolokasi braised with celery and lemon through the winter, artichokes and broad beans in spring, koupepia rolled with rice and herbs and no mince at all, aubergines a dozen ways, and horta, the mixed wild greens that get boiled and dressed and eaten by the plateful.

Dressing pulses the Cypriot way

The ratio that matters is more olive oil and more lemon than feels reasonable, added while the beans are still hot, plus enough salt to make them taste of something. Roughly 60 ml of oil and the juice of a whole lemon to 400 g of drained cooked beans. Do it hot, let it sit twenty minutes, taste again. Pulses dressed cold stay bland no matter how much you add later.

Roast Pumpkin Soup with Toasted Pepitas

Roasting the pumpkin instead of boiling it is the difference between a nice soup and one people ask you about. It concentrates the sugars and gives you the browned edges a pot of simmering water never will. Autumn here is full of squash and sweet potato, and both work.

Method

  1. Roast the vegetables

    Heat the oven to 220°C, or 200°C fan-forced. Toss the pumpkin, onion and unpeeled garlic with the oil and salt and spread over two trays. Roast for 30 to 35 minutes until the edges are properly browned, not just soft.

  2. Squeeze out the garlic

    Squeeze the roasted garlic out of its skins. Roasted whole, it goes sweet and mellow instead of sharp.

  3. Blend with hot stock

    Tip everything into a large pot, add the cumin, nutmeg and hot stock, and blend with a stick blender until completely smooth. Add stock a little at a time until it is the thickness you want.

  4. Warm and finish

    Bring back to a gentle simmer for 5 minutes. Stir in the lemon juice, then taste and adjust the salt. The lemon is not optional, it is what stops the soup tasting like sweet baby food.

  5. Serve

    Ladle into bowls and top with toasted pepitas, a swirl of cream and chilli flakes if using.

Cook's tips

  • Do not crowd the trays. Two trays with space beats one tray piled high every time. Crowded pumpkin steams and you lose the browning that the whole recipe depends on.
  • Add stock gradually. You can always thin a thick soup and you cannot easily thicken a thin one. Start with 750 ml and work up.
  • Toast pepitas in a dry pan. Three minutes over medium heat, shaking constantly, until they start to pop. They burn in the oven while you are not looking.
  • Freeze it flat. Portion into zip bags, lay them flat in the freezer, and they thaw in twenty minutes in a sink of warm water. A pinch of cinnamon on reheating is very Cypriot and suits it better than you would expect.

Storage and make ahead

Keeps 4 days in the fridge and 3 months in the freezer. Freeze without the cream and add it when reheating, since dairy can split on thawing. Reheat gently and stir often, the thickness makes it catch on the base.

Vegan version. Use vegetable stock and coconut cream. Everything else is already plant-based.

Allergens. Contains dairy if using cream. Gluten free if your stock is. Read the packaging in your own kitchen, stock cubes often contain gluten and celery.

Green Goddess Salad with Charred Broccolini

A herb dressing that uses the stalks as well as the leaves, so a whole bunch of parsley gets eaten instead of half of it going to slime in the drawer. Buy the parsley at the laiki, where it comes in bunches twice the size for half the money.

Method

  1. Blitz the dressing

    Put the parsley, basil, spring onions, olive oil, lemon juice, garlic and salt in a blender and blitz for 60 seconds until bright green and smooth. Include the fine parsley stems, they carry a lot of flavour.

  2. Char the broccolini

    Heat a griddle or heavy frying pan until very hot. Toss the broccolini with the olive oil and cook for 4 minutes, turning once, until blistered in patches and still crisp at the stem.

  3. Cool it slightly

    Move the broccolini to a board and cut into 4 cm lengths. Let it cool for 5 minutes so it does not wilt the lettuce.

  4. Build the salad

    Toss the cos, cucumber and tomatoes in a large bowl with half the dressing. Add the broccolini and toss again.

  5. Finish

    Scatter with toasted almonds and serve the remaining dressing on the side. Dress at the last moment, cos wilts fast.

Cook's tips

  • Blend the dressing cold and fast. A long blend heats the herbs and turns the dressing khaki. Sixty seconds is plenty, and a few ice cubes in the blender helps, which from June onwards here is not optional.
  • Dry the lettuce properly. Water on the leaves stops the dressing sticking and leaves a puddle in the bottom of the bowl. A salad spinner earns its cupboard space.
  • Save the broccolini ends. The woody bottom 2 cm is good chopped fine and thrown into a stir-fry or a stock. Do not put it in the salad. If broccolini is nowhere to be found, wild asparagus in spring or the tender inner stems of a spring cabbage char just as well.

Storage and make ahead

The dressing keeps 3 days refrigerated in a jar with a thin film of oil on top to stop it browning. Broccolini can be charred a day ahead. Assembled and dressed salad does not keep at all, so build only what you will eat.

Vegan version. Already vegan as written. Some versions add anchovy or parmesan, which this one does not.

Allergens. Contains tree nuts (almonds). Leave them out and use toasted pepitas for a nut free version. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Warm Grain Bowl with Roast Vegetables and Tahini

Built to be eaten hot on Sunday night and cold from a container on Tuesday. The grains are dressed warm so they absorb the lemon instead of just carrying it. Pourgouri, the coarse Cypriot bulgur, is the cheapest and best grain you can put in it.

For the roast vegetables

To dress and finish

Method

  1. Roast the vegetables

    Heat the oven to 220°C, or 200°C fan-forced. Toss the courgette, aubergine, pepper and onion with the olive oil, cumin and salt, spread over two trays and roast for 30 minutes, turning once.

  2. Cook the grains

    Bring the stock to the boil, add the grains, cover and simmer according to the packet: about 10 minutes for pearl couscous, 20 for freekeh, 12 for burghul. Drain any excess.

  3. Make the dressing

    Whisk the tahini and lemon juice. It will seize into a paste. Add the cold water a spoonful at a time, whisking, until it becomes a pourable cream.

  4. Dress the grains warm

    Tip the hot grains into a large bowl and stir through a third of the tahini dressing and half the parsley. Warm grains absorb dressing, cold grains just get coated.

  5. Combine and serve

    Fold the roast vegetables through, top with the remaining dressing, parsley and toasted pepitas, and season again to taste.

Cook's tips

  • Cut the aubergine bigger than everything else. It shrinks by about half in the oven. Small cubes disappear entirely.
  • Toast the grains first if you have two minutes. Dry-fry pearl couscous, freekeh or pourgouri in the pot for 2 minutes before the stock goes in. It adds a nutty depth for almost no effort, and it is how pourgouri is started here anyway.
  • Make double the dressing. It keeps a week and turns any tray of roast vegetables into a meal.

Storage and make ahead

Keeps 4 days in the fridge and is designed to be eaten cold. Dress only half if you want to keep some for later in the week, since the grains keep absorbing. Do not freeze, the vegetables go watery.

Vegan version. Already vegan as written. Check your stock does not contain honey or dairy solids.

Allergens. Contains sesame (tahini) and gluten (couscous, freekeh, burghul). For a gluten free version use quinoa or brown rice. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Summer Berry and Melon Platter with Lime and Mint

The dessert that survives a 38°C afternoon. Nothing to set, nothing to bake, and it will sit on the table for an hour without suffering, which is exactly what the end of a long Cypriot lunch needs.

For the lime syrup

Method

  1. Make the syrup

    Heat the sugar and water in a small saucepan, stirring until the sugar dissolves, then simmer for 2 minutes. Take it off the heat, add the lime zest and juice, and cool completely.

  2. Cut the melon last

    Cut the watermelon and cantaloupe into wedges no more than an hour before serving. Cut melon weeps and softens quickly.

  3. Arrange, do not toss

    Lay the melon wedges out on a large platter and pile the berries and cherries into the gaps. Tossing bruises the berries and stains everything pink.

  4. Dress at the table

    Spoon the cooled lime syrup over the fruit and scatter with torn mint just before serving.

Cook's tips

  • Leave the rind on the watermelon. It gives people something to hold, it stops the wedges collapsing, and it means far less juice on the table.
  • Do not wash berries until you use them. Water on strawberries in the fridge turns them mouldy within a day. Wash them at the last minute and dry them on a tea towel.
  • Cold fruit tastes of less. Take everything except the melon out of the fridge 20 minutes before serving. Melon is better cold. In July, watermelon straight from the fridge with a block of halloumi beside it is a whole course on its own.

Storage and make ahead

The lime syrup keeps 2 weeks in the fridge and is worth making ahead. Fruit is best cut on the day. Leftovers keep 1 day covered and are excellent blended into a smoothie. Watermelon does not freeze well, but berries do.

Vegan version. Already vegan and gluten free as written.

Allergens. No common allergens. Read the packaging in your own kitchen if you add anything bought, such as coconut yoghurt.

Overnight Oats with Grated Apple and Cinnamon

Five jars made on Sunday night covers the working week. Grated apple is the difference between this and the claggy grey version people give up on after a fortnight. Carob syrup instead of the maple is the local move, and a better one.

Method

  1. Mix the base

    Combine the oats, milk, yoghurt, grated apple, cinnamon, maple syrup, chia seeds and salt in a large bowl. Stir well, the chia will try to clump.

  2. Rest for five minutes, then stir again

    Chia seeds swell unevenly. A second stir after five minutes stops you finding a solid lump of them at the bottom of a jar on Wednesday.

  3. Divide into jars

    Spoon into five clean jars with lids, leaving 2 cm of headroom. Refrigerate overnight, or at least 6 hours.

  4. Top on the day

    Add nuts and berries in the morning rather than the night before, so the nuts stay crisp. Frozen berries can go on straight from the freezer and will thaw by morning tea.

Cook's tips

  • Rolled oats, never quick oats. Quick oats are pre-broken and turn to paste overnight. Traditional rolled oats hold their shape for days.
  • Grate the apple with the skin on. The skin adds texture and stops the whole jar being uniformly soft, and grating rather than dicing means it distributes through every spoonful.
  • The salt matters. Half a teaspoon across five jars is not enough to taste salty, but without it the oats taste flat and you will keep adding syrup to compensate. Cyprus sea salt, crushed between your fingers.

Storage and make ahead

Keeps 4 days refrigerated, which is exactly the working week if you make it on Sunday night. After day four the apple starts to brown and the texture softens. Do not freeze.

Vegan version. Use a plant milk and coconut yoghurt. Maple syrup is already vegan, but check if you swap it for honey.

Allergens. Contains gluten (oats, unless certified gluten free) and tree nuts. Dairy if using cow's milk or Greek yoghurt. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Frozen Berry, Banana and Oat Smoothie

Oats instead of protein powder and frozen banana instead of ice, so it is thick without being watered down. One blender jug, and the whole thing takes less time than the toaster.

None

2 serves
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Gluten, dairy optional

Ingredients

Serves
2

Metric measures. One metric cup is 250 ml, one tablespoon is 20 ml. US cups are 240 ml and imperial cups 284 ml.

Metric 250 ml cups.

For the smoothie

0 of 7 ticked

Method

  1. Blitz the oats dry first

    Blend the rolled oats alone for 20 seconds until they look like coarse flour. This stops the finished smoothie being gritty, which is the usual complaint about oats in a blender.

  2. Add liquids before solids

    Pour in the milk and yoghurt, then add the frozen banana and berries on top. Liquid at the bottom lets the blades get going instead of jamming.

  3. Blend in bursts

    Blend on high for 45 to 60 seconds, stopping once to scrape down the sides. Long continuous blending warms the mixture and thins it.

  4. Taste and adjust

    Add the maple syrup only if the berries are sharp, and the pinch of salt regardless. Blend for another 5 seconds and pour.

Cook's tips

  • Freeze overripe bananas. Peel and slice them before freezing, never freeze them whole in the skin. A bag of frozen banana slices in the freezer is the foundation of every good smoothie.
  • Frozen fruit, not ice. Ice dilutes as it blends and the drink is watery within two minutes. Frozen fruit keeps it thick and keeps the flavour. Freeze cut figs and grapes in September when the laiki is giving them away.
  • Frozen berries are usually cheaper and often better. Berries are an import here and fresh ones arrive tired. Frozen fruit is snap frozen at harvest, so outside the short local strawberry season in spring it wins on both taste and price.

Storage and make ahead

Drink immediately, it separates within about 20 minutes. You can portion the dry and frozen ingredients into bags ahead of time and keep them in the freezer, then tip a bag into the blender with the milk. Do not refrigerate a blended smoothie overnight.

Vegan version. Use a plant milk, coconut yoghurt and maple syrup.

Allergens. Contains gluten (oats, unless certified gluten free) and dairy if using cow's milk or Greek yoghurt. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

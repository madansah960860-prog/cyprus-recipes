Quick Weeknight Dinners

Everything here is on the table in forty minutes from a standing start, using ingredients you can pick up in one trip to Alphamega, Lidl or the laiki on the way home. No marinating, no special order, no second shop.

What counts as a weeknight dinner here

Forty minutes, measured honestly. The clock starts when you walk into the kitchen and stops when the food is on the table, and it includes chopping, waiting for water to boil, and the four minutes spent looking for the good pan. Anything that needs marinating, resting or a special trip lives elsewhere on the site.

The second rule is the washing up. Every dish below needs at most one pan and one board. A twenty five minute recipe that leaves eleven things in the sink is not a quick dinner, it is a quick cook followed by a slow clean.

The third rule is unwritten and Cypriot: nobody eats dinner at six. Plenty of households here sit down at nine, later still in July when the whole day shifts to the cool end of it. That is an argument for food that holds. Three of the five recipes below are as good at room temperature as they are hot, which means you can cook when you have time rather than when people are hungry.

There are five complete recipes on this page. Each one has a tickable ingredient checklist, a method, storage notes and the things we got wrong while testing it. The weeknight shelf

Fast cooking is mostly a shopping problem. If these are in the house on Sunday, four of the five recipes below are already half done, and so are most of the dinners that never make it onto a website.

Six things that turn into dinner

A block of halloumi, a jar of good olives, a tin of black-eyed beans, a bag of pourgouri, a lemon and a bunch of flat leaf parsley. Fry the halloumi and put it on anything. Dress warm pourgouri hard with lemon and oil. Tip the beans into a pan with greens and garlic. None of that needs a recipe, and all of it is under ten minutes.

25 minutes

Prawn and Avocado Rice Bowls

Raw prawns cook in ninety seconds, which makes them the fastest protein in the shop. Everything else here happens while the rice steams. Buy them already peeled if you want this closer to twenty minutes.

Method

  1. Start the rice

    Put the rinsed rice and water in a saucepan with a tight lid. Bring to the boil, drop to the lowest heat, cover and cook for 12 minutes. Turn the heat off and leave the lid on for 5 more minutes. Do not lift the lid before then.

  2. Mix the dressing

    Whisk the lime juice, soy sauce, olive oil and sugar until the sugar dissolves. Add the chilli if you are using it. Taste and adjust, it should be sharp enough to make you blink.

  3. Prepare the vegetables

    Shred the cabbage, slice the cucumber, halve the tomatoes and cut the avocado just before serving so it does not brown.

  4. Cook the prawns hard and fast

    Pat the prawns very dry. Heat the neutral oil in a frying pan until it shimmers, then cook the prawns in a single layer for 60 to 90 seconds each side, until they turn opaque and curl into a loose C shape. A tight O means overcooked.

  5. Build the bowls

    Fluff the rice with a fork and divide it between four bowls. Arrange the vegetables and prawns in sections on top rather than mixing everything, then spoon over the dressing at the table.

Cook's tips

  • Buy raw prawns, not cooked. The pink ones are already at their limit and turn rubbery in the pan. You want the grey translucent ones, and any fishmonger here will peel them for you while you wait.
  • Frozen prawns are fine. Thaw them in a colander in the fridge overnight or under cold running water for ten minutes, then dry them thoroughly. Never thaw in warm water.
  • Salt the cabbage. A pinch of salt on the shredded cabbage five minutes before serving softens it just enough that it stops being a mouthful of raw vegetable. Cypriot winter cabbage is sweet enough that this is all it needs.

Storage and make ahead

The dressing keeps for a week in the fridge. Cooked rice keeps for 1 day only, refrigerated within an hour of cooking, and should be reheated until steaming. Prawns are best eaten immediately. Cut avocado does not keep.

Allergens. Contains crustacean and soy, and gluten via the soy sauce. Use tamari for a gluten free version. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Pesto Pasta Salad with Semi-Dried Tomatoes

The recipe we make most often, because it is better cold on day two than hot on day one. It goes to work, it goes to the beach in August, and it uses a jar of pesto without apologising for it.

To loosen and finish

Method

  1. Cook the pasta properly salted

    Bring a large pot of water to the boil and salt it until it tastes like a mild broth. Cook the pasta to the packet time, not a minute less, because a cold pasta salad wants pasta that is fully cooked rather than al dente.

  2. Save the water

    Scoop out 60 ml of the cooking water before you drain. This is what turns the pesto into a dressing rather than a paste.

  3. Dress the pasta while hot

    Drain but do not rinse. Tip the pasta straight back into the pot, add the pesto, lemon juice, olive oil and the reserved water, and toss hard. Hot pasta absorbs dressing, cold pasta just wears it.

  4. Cool for ten minutes

    Spread the dressed pasta on a tray to cool for 10 minutes. Adding leaves to hot pasta cooks them into a khaki mess.

  5. Fold through the rest

    Add the semi-dried tomatoes, bocconcini, pine nuts and rocket. Fold gently, season with salt flakes and pepper, and serve at room temperature.

Cook's tips

  • Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan, not the oven. They go from pale to burnt in about fifteen seconds and you cannot watch an oven properly. Shake the pan constantly and tip them out the moment they smell nutty.
  • Buy pesto from the fridge, not the shelf. Refrigerated pesto tastes green. Shelf-stable jars taste of the jar. If a shelf jar is all there is, wake it up with extra lemon and a handful of torn basil, which grows on every Cypriot balcony from April onwards.
  • Undress a third of it if you want leftovers. Keep some pasta plain, and dress it the next morning. Pesto dulls in the fridge overnight, so a fresh spoonful through it makes lunch much better.

Storage and make ahead

Keeps in an airtight container in the fridge for 3 days. The rocket will wilt, so add half at serving and half fresh on day two. Bring to room temperature for twenty minutes before eating, because cold dulls the basil. Do not freeze.

Allergens. Contains gluten, dairy (pesto and bocconcini) and tree nuts (pine nuts and often cashew in bought pesto). Read the label on your pesto, many contain cashews. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

20 minutes

Smashed Avocado with Poached Eggs and Dukkah

Breakfast for dinner is a legitimate weeknight strategy. This is the cafe version minus the queue and the €14, and it hinges entirely on a gentle pot of water and eggs that are actually fresh.

For the eggs and toast

Method

  1. Get the water ready first

    Fill a wide saucepan with 8 cm of water, add the vinegar, and bring it to a bare simmer. You want the surface trembling, not bubbling. A rolling boil shreds the whites.

  2. Smash the avocado

    Scoop the avocados into a bowl, add the lemon juice and salt flakes, and mash with a fork until chunky. Do not blend it smooth. Texture is the point.

  3. Crack each egg into a cup first

    Crack one egg into a small cup, lower the cup to the water surface and tip it in gently. Repeat with the rest, keeping them apart. Cook for 3 minutes for a runny yolk, 4 for jammy.

  4. Toast the bread while the eggs cook

    Toast the sourdough well, so it can stand up to wet toppings. Rub the hot toast with a cut clove of garlic if you like.

  5. Assemble

    Spread the avocado thickly on the toast, add a handful of spinach or rocket, and lift each egg out with a slotted spoon, resting it briefly on paper towel to drain. Sit the eggs on top, scatter with dukkah and serve with lemon wedges.

Cook's tips

  • Fresh eggs poach neatly, older eggs do not. The white of a fresh egg holds together. If yours have been in the fridge for a fortnight, crack them into a sieve first and let the loose watery white drain away. Village eggs from a roadside stall are usually days old rather than weeks.
  • Drain the eggs before they touch the toast. Ten seconds on paper towel is the difference between crisp toast and soggy toast.
  • Ripen avocados in a paper bag with a banana. Two days in a bag does what four days on the windowsill will not. Once ripe, move them to the fridge to stop the clock. Paphos avocados run from about November to March and are worth waiting for.

Storage and make ahead

Not a keeping dish. Smashed avocado browns within a few hours even with lemon juice, so make only what you will eat. Poached eggs can be cooked ahead, chilled in cold water and reheated for 45 seconds in hot water, which is worth knowing if you are cooking for six.

Allergens. Contains egg, gluten (sourdough), sesame and tree nuts (dukkah usually contains hazelnut or almond and sesame). Read the dukkah label. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Grilled Salmon with Herb and Caper Dressing

Salmon is imported here and priced accordingly, so it is worth knowing the two rules that stop it being ruined: a dry surface and a pan you do not touch for four minutes. The dressing is made while the fish rests.

Method

  1. Dry the skin and salt it

    Pat the salmon skin bone dry with paper towel, then salt it generously and leave it uncovered for 10 minutes. Moisture is the enemy of crisp skin.

  2. Make the dressing

    Combine the capers, parsley, dill, olive oil, lemon juice and eschalot in a bowl. Let it sit while you cook, the eschalot softens in the acid.

  3. Heat the pan properly

    Put a heavy frying pan over medium-high heat with the neutral oil. Wait until the oil shimmers and moves like water. A cold pan means the skin sticks and tears.

  4. Skin side down, and leave it

    Lay the fillets skin down, pressing each one flat with a spatula for 10 seconds so the skin makes full contact. Cook for 4 minutes without moving them. The colour will creep up the sides of the fillet.

  5. Flip once

    Turn the fillets and cook for 90 seconds to 2 minutes more. The centre should still be slightly translucent. Salmon keeps cooking off the heat.

  6. Rest and dress

    Rest for 3 minutes on a warm plate, skin up so it stays crisp. Serve on crushed potatoes with the dressing spooned over the fish and the plate.

Cook's tips

  • Buy fillets of even thickness. A tapered tail piece cooks in half the time of a thick centre cut, so a mixed tray will never finish together. Ask for centre cuts. A thick fillet of local lavraki or tsipoura works the same way and costs less.
  • Rinse the capers. Salted capers need a proper soak and brined ones need a rinse, or the dressing turns aggressively salty and there is no taking it back. Cypriot caper shoots preserved in brine, the whole stem rather than the bud, are excellent here chopped through.
  • Frozen salmon works. Thaw it overnight in the fridge on a rack over a plate, then dry it very well. It will not crisp quite as hard as fresh, but the dressing covers a lot of ground.

Storage and make ahead

The dressing can be made 2 days ahead, without the herbs, which are stirred in on the day. Cooked salmon keeps 2 days in the fridge and is excellent cold, flaked through a salad. Do not reheat it, the texture goes chalky.

Allergens. Contains fish. Naturally gluten free and dairy free as written. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Tomato and Basil Bruschetta on Sourdough

Only worth making between June and September, when Cypriot tomatoes actually taste of something. Out of season the whole dish is a slice of toast with a disappointment on it.

For the base

Method

  1. Salt the tomatoes first

    Chop the tomatoes into rough pieces, put them in a bowl with the salt flakes, and leave them for 10 minutes. They will release a pool of sweet liquid. This step is the whole recipe.

  2. Dress them

    Add the olive oil and vinegar to the tomatoes and their juices. Do not drain the liquid away, it becomes the sauce.

  3. Grill the bread

    Grill or toast the sourdough until it is properly dark at the edges, then rub one side of each hot slice with the cut garlic clove. The rough toast grates the garlic into the surface. If the coals are still going from the weekend, do it over those.

  4. Assemble at the last minute

    Tear the basil into the tomatoes, spoon the mixture onto the toast and pour any remaining juices over the top. Serve immediately, before the bread softens.

Cook's tips

  • Never refrigerate the tomatoes. Below about 12°C they stop producing the compounds that make them taste like tomatoes, and the texture turns mealy. Keep them on the bench, stem side down, out of direct sun.
  • Tear the basil, do not chop it. A knife bruises the leaves and turns the edges black within a minute. Tearing keeps them green.
  • Day-old bread is better. Fresh sourdough goes soggy fast. Bread a day or two old toasts drier and holds the topping longer. A dense village loaf is the best thing you can put under this.

Storage and make ahead

The dressed tomatoes can sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours and only improve. Do not refrigerate them. Assembled bruschetta must be eaten straight away, so toast in batches if you are feeding a crowd.

Allergens. Contains gluten. Naturally dairy free and vegan as written. Use a gluten free loaf if needed. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

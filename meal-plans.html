Weekly Meal Plans

Five dinners built around one Saturday at the laiki and one stop at the supermarket, with the list already written and grouped the way the shop is laid out. The plans are free, they use recipes that are already on the site, and there is nothing to sign up for.

How these plans are built

Every plan follows the same three rules. First, overlap the ingredients, so a bunch of coriander or half a tub of tahini gets used across two or three meals instead of going soft in the fridge. Second, front-load the effort onto the one night that has time. Third, keep at least two dinners under thirty minutes, because a plan that assumes you have an hour every evening is a plan you abandon by Wednesday.

Then there is a fourth rule that is particular to cooking here. In summer, the oven stays off. Between June and September a Cypriot kitchen runs on the hob, the grill pan and the foukou out on the balcony after dark. From November the oven comes back and the tapsi does most of the work. The plans follow that, because a July plan full of roast trays is a plan nobody cooks.

Quantities assume four adult portions per dinner. If you are cooking for two, most recipes on the site have a servings scaler that recalculates every quantity, and the leftovers of the longer cooks are usually better on day two anyway.

Costs are given in euros as an indication only. Prices move week to week and the gap between the laiki on a Saturday morning and an air-conditioned supermarket on a Sunday evening is real money. What the plans do is favour cheaper cuts, whatever is in season, and pantry staples, which is where the saving actually lives.

This week: high summer, five dinners

Published 17/08/2026. Built around what is cheap and everywhere in August: tomatoes, aubergines, courgettes, watermelon, and fish off the counter rather than meat out of the oven. Indicative cost for the week, feeding four: around €85.

Why these five together. Three kilos of August tomatoes do most of the week: raw in Monday's bowls, slow-roasted on Tuesday while the pasta water comes up, charred on Thursday, in the salsa on Sunday. One bunch of flat leaf parsley covers Wednesday and Thursday. The coals lit for Sunday are still hot afterwards, so char the aubergines and peppers for Thursday then and keep them in the fridge in oil. That is Thursday reduced to fifteen minutes.

The timeline

  • Saturday morning. The laiki, early, before the heat. Tomatoes, aubergines, courgettes, peppers, herbs, watermelon. Supermarket afterwards for everything in a packet.
  • Sunday evening. Light the foukou for the chops. While the coals burn down, char Thursday's aubergines and peppers alongside and store them in oil.
  • Monday. Rice on, everything else raw and cold. Twenty five minutes, no heat in the kitchen.
  • Tuesday. Tomatoes into a low oven with oil and oregano as you walk in. They are done when the pasta is.
  • Wednesday. Salt the fish as soon as you get home and leave it while you make the dressing.
  • Thursday. Whip the feta with a little yoghurt and lemon. The vegetables are already charred. Bread and a bowl.

A nistisima week

For the weeks of Lent, for the Friday before a feast, or for anyone who simply wants a week without meat or dairy.

Nistisima cooking is not a modern diet trend here. It is the oldest pattern of eating on the island, and it is the reason Cypriot kitchens are so good at pulses, greens and oil. On fasting days there is no meat, no dairy, no eggs and no fish with a backbone. Shellfish, octopus and calamari are allowed, which is why the tavernas fill with octopus during Lent. On the strictest days olive oil goes too, though most households keep it.

The useful thing about a nistisima week is that it is cheap. A week of louvi, chickpeas, greens and pourgouri costs a fraction of a week built on meat, and nobody at the table feels short-changed.

Five nistisima dinners

  • Monday. Louvi me lahana — black-eyed beans with chard, finished with lemon and a hard pour of olive oil. Cook double.
  • Tuesday. Warm grain bowl with roast vegetables and tahini, built on pourgouri instead of the grain listed.
  • Wednesday. Octopus braised slowly with red wine and small onions, with bread. The one indulgence of the week, and still allowed.
  • Thursday. Roast pumpkin, chickpea and rocket salad, dairy left out.
  • Friday. Yesterday's louvi, warmed through with the rest of the greens, and a plate of olives, tomatoes and bread.

The shop. 500 g dried louvi, 500 g chickpeas, 500 g coarse pourgouri, 2 kg chard or spinach, 1 kg onions, 1 octopus, tahini, lemons, olives, bread. Around €35 for four people for five dinners.

Check the label if you are fasting strictly. Some shop-bought breads and pastries contain milk or egg.

Summer meze for eight

A long Sunday lunch on the foukou, built around one protein and three things made ahead and served at room temperature. Cypriot hosting in one table.

  • Pork chops with a charred salsa, doubled
  • Grilled corn with lemon and smoked paprika butter
  • Charred vegetable platter with whipped feta
  • Tomato and basil bruschetta to start
  • Watermelon, figs and halloumi to finish

Shop for eight. 8 pork chops, 6 corn cobs, 3 courgettes, 4 red peppers, 2 aubergines, 3 red onions, 250 g feta, 250 g halloumi, 300 g yoghurt, 1.5 kg tomatoes, 2 village loaves, 1 watermelon, 500 g figs, 500 g grapes, 4 lemons, 2 bunches mint, 1 bunch parsley, 1 bunch basil, charcoal. Around €70.

Winter week, the oven back on

Five dinners for December or January, when citrus is everywhere, kolokasi is in the crates and nobody minds the oven running for three hours.

  • Slow-cooked beef ragù on Sunday, six portions, two frozen
  • Roast pumpkin soup with village bread
  • Chicken tray bake with potatoes and plenty of lemon
  • Chicken soup, made with the tray bake's carcass
  • Cauliflower, kolokasi and a bowl of oranges to finish

The overlap. One chicken gives you Wednesday's tray bake and Thursday's soup. One pumpkin covers the soup twice. A five kilo net of oranges in January costs about €4 and lasts the week.

Cooking for two, with lunches

Three cooks that cover five dinners and three lunches, using the servings scaler on each recipe page.

  • Sunday: beef ragù at the full six portions. Two dinners plus two lunches.
  • Tuesday: warm grain bowl at four portions. One dinner plus two lunches.
  • Thursday: prawn and avocado rice bowls scaled to two.
  • Breakfast all week: overnight oats with carob syrup, five jars made Sunday night.

Note. Do not scale the ragù down. It freezes well and the effort is identical, which is the whole point of cooking it.

The Thursday email

One email a week, written on Wednesday night after the cooking is done. The new recipes, what is worth buying at the laiki this weekend, and the shopping list for the current meal plan. Nothing else.

What is actually in it

  • The two or three recipes published that week, and a line on why we cooked them.
  • What is in season and what is cheap right now, taken from the market that week rather than a chart.
  • The week's meal plan and its shopping list, grouped by aisle.
  • One short answer to a question a reader has sent in.
  • Occasionally one clearly labelled sponsor. Never more than one, never dressed up as editorial.

About five minutes to read. Sent Thursday mornings, Cyprus time.

The year, one Thursday at a time

The email follows the island rather than the calendar of some other country. What lands in your inbox in July is not what lands in January, because what is on the stalls is not the same and neither is your kitchen.

  1. Spring, March to May

    The busiest weeks of the year. Artichokes, broad beans, agrelia, fresh garlic, green almonds, strawberries and loquats. Flaounes before Easter, with the plaiting explained properly, and nistisima cooking through the fasting weeks. Everything is green and nothing needs long cooking.

  2. Summer, June to August

    Thirty-five degrees inland and nobody wants an oven on. Tomatoes, cucumbers, watermelon, Kyperounta cherries, figs, peaches, aubergines, courgettes and bamies. Grilling, meze for the balcony, cold dishes, and a run of recipes that never go above a hob ring.

  3. Autumn, September to November

    The best eating of the year. The grape and wine harvest, palouzes and soutzoukos, pomegranates, quinces, carobs, Troodos walnuts and the olive pressing. The oven comes back on, slow pork returns, and preserving starts: spoon sweets, pickles and syrups.

  4. Winter, December to February

    Mild on the coast, snow up on Troodos. Oranges, mandarins, lemons and bitter oranges, cauliflower, cabbage, kolokasi, spinach, wild greens and Paphos avocados. Avgolemono, trachanas, baked kolokasi with pork, Christmas cooking and the citrus baking that carries January.

Two weeks are different every year: the week before Easter, which is all flaounes and fasting dishes, and the second week of August, when we send almost nothing because nobody is cooking.

How we handle your consent and your details

Marketing email in Cyprus is governed by the GDPR (EU) 2016/679 and Cyprus Law 125(I)/2018, together with the national rules on electronic communications. Three things follow from that. We would do all three anyway.

  1. Express consent

    You tick the consent box on this page, then confirm by clicking a link in an email we send you. We do not add people from contact forms, competitions or bought lists, and we do not rely on consent you did not knowingly give. Consent is recorded, and it is as easy to withdraw as it was to give.

  2. Clear sender identity

    Every email states that it comes from Meraki Table Media Ltd, includes our registration and VAT numbers and our Limassol postal address, and gives a reply address that a person reads.

  3. A working unsubscribe

    One click, in every email, free. It takes effect immediately, and we do not ask you to log in or answer a survey first. Unsubscribing removes you from the list entirely rather than pausing you.

What we collect and why

Your email address, because there is no email without it. Your first name if you give it, so the email can address you properly. Your chosen frequency. We also record the date, time and page on which you gave consent, because we have to be able to demonstrate that consent was given.

Our email service provider stores this data and may process it outside the European Economic Area, in which case the transfer is covered by the safeguards the GDPR requires. Your rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and how to exercise them, are set out in the privacy policy. If you are not satisfied with how we answer, you can complain to the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection in Nicosia.

We do not sell or rent the list. A sponsor who books a placement in the newsletter never receives your address. They pay for a labelled block inside the email and nothing else.

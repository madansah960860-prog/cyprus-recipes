September in Cyprus, the turn into autumn

Cook what the island is giving this month

September is the harvest. Grapes come down from the Troodos villages and the presses run, while everything on the coast is still hot enough to taste of July. Aubergines, courgettes and okra are at their cheapest, the last good tomatoes are here, and the first pomegranates and prickly pears turn up on the stalls long before the citrus arrives in December.

Our produce guide runs on the Cypriot calendar, so summer means June to August and winter means oranges, kolokasi and wild greens rather than stone fruit. Buy heavily now and put some away: this is the month for spoon sweets, for drying oregano off the stem, and for roasting and freezing peppers while they cost almost nothing.

At the laiki this week

Go before nine and walk the whole row once before you buy anything. Ask for the small knobbly okra rather than the long smooth ones, they hold their shape in the pot. Village tomatoes sold soft and split are the ones to buy for sauce and usually cost half as much. Take your own bag and small notes, and expect about €1.50 a kilo for courgettes while the season is at its peak.

What we are for

A table worth sitting at for three hours

Cypriot food is not difficult. It is patient. The halloumi goes on dry, the bread goes round the table by hand, and the second plate of everything arrives whether you asked for it or not.

We write for the kitchen as it actually is in this country: a small hob, a long hot summer, a jar of carob syrup at the back of the cupboard and a tin of good oil somebody in the family sent down from the village. The recipes are precise because precision respects your time and your money, not because cooking should feel like an exam.

So there is no method that eats a Sunday for no reason, and no ingredient ordered from abroad when something grown twenty minutes away does the job better. We do not call a dish traditional when we have changed it, and we do not call it ours when a yiayia in Lefkara taught it to us.

And there is no paywall. If a recipe is on this site, you can cook it tonight for nothing.

Who pays for all this

We sell you nothing in order to read a recipe. There is no paywall on any cooking content on Meraki Table and there will not be one. A tested recipe site still costs money to run, so here is where ours comes from, set out plainly.

  1. Display advertising

    We sell advertising space through partners including Google. Ads sit below the content, carry an advertising label, and never cover a method step, an ingredient list or a button.

  2. Affiliate commissions

    Some links to pans, thermometers and pantry staples earn us a small commission if you buy. The disclosure sits beside the link itself rather than only in the footer, and you pay exactly the same price either way.

  3. Cookbooks and seasonal bundles

    We sell downloadable PDF cookbooks and seasonal meal-plan bundles, priced in euro. Buying one is optional, and nothing that was free on the site is ever moved into a paid file.

  4. Optional membership

    Meraki Table Membership is €5.50 a month for ad-free browsing, printable recipe cards and saved collections.

  5. Sponsored recipes and newsletter slots

    Now and then a producer pays us to develop a recipe. Those pages carry a sponsorship label at the very top, above the photograph, and the testing standard does not change. Newsletter sponsorship is labelled the same way inside the email.

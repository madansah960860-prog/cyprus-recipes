Advertising & Affiliate Disclosure

Every way this site makes money, written plainly. If you only read one section, read the first one.

Last updated 14 September 2026. Version 2.0.

Please note. This legal copy is a starting template and must be reviewed by a Cyprus-qualified lawyer before launch. It is not legal advice.

1. How this site is funded, in one paragraph

Recipes on Meraki Table are free to read and there is no paywall on any cooking content. The site is paid for by display advertising, by small affiliate commissions when readers buy equipment or ingredients through some of our links, by downloadable cookbooks and seasonal meal-plan bundles priced in euros, by an optional monthly membership that removes advertising, and by occasional sponsored recipes and newsletter sponsorships that are labelled as such at the top of the page or the top of the placement. That is the entire list. There is nothing else, and there is no arrangement we have not told you about.

2. The rules we work to

Commercial communication in Cyprus is governed by the Consumer Protection Law of Cyprus and by EU consumer protection law, in particular Directive 2005/29/EC on unfair commercial practices, which prohibits misleading actions and misleading omissions, and expressly forbids using editorial content in the media to promote a product where the trader has paid for it without making that clear. Two further rules bite here: Directive 2000/31/EC on electronic commerce requires commercial communication to be identifiable as such and to identify who is behind it, and Directive 2011/83/EU requires clear pre-contractual information before you buy anything.

Enforcement in Cyprus sits with the Consumer Protection Service of the Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry. The short version of all of it: a commercial relationship must be obvious to a reader at the moment it matters, without them having to hunt for it. That is the standard we hold ourselves to on this page and on every page.

3. Display advertising

We sell advertising space on the site through advertising partners, including Google. Advertising pays for most of the cost of testing, photographing and publishing recipes.

Our rules for ourselves:

  • Every advertising unit is labelled Advertisement, in the reader's language, above the unit.
  • Ad units are visually distinct from editorial content, with their own border and background. Nothing on this site is designed to be mistaken for a recipe.
  • No advertising sits above the main content at the top of the page, and none overlays a recipe, a button or the navigation.
  • Space is reserved for each unit so the page does not jump while it loads.
  • No pop-ups, no pop-unders, no interstitials on entry, no automatic downloads, no automatic redirects, and no video that plays with sound.
  • No advertising at all on our legal pages or the error page.
  • Total ad density is kept well below content density on every page.

Personalised advertising runs only where you have consented to advertising cookies, as the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC and the GDPR require. Refuse, and you still see ads, selected without your browsing history. How to give or withdraw that consent is in the Cookie Policy.

We do not choose which specific ads appear. Those are selected by the advertising partner. If you see an ad on this site that is offensive, misleading or broken, tell us and we will have it blocked.

4. Affiliate links

Some links to kitchen equipment and pantry ingredients are affiliate links. If you click one and buy something, we may receive a small commission from the retailer. The price you pay is exactly the same, and the commission comes out of the retailer's margin.

How we disclose them

A commission is a commercial relationship, and under the unfair commercial practices rules leaving it out is a misleading omission whether or not anything we wrote was untrue. So we disclose in three places, before you reach the link rather than after:

  1. At the link

    A short line of text directly beside or beneath the link itself saying it is an affiliate link. Not a symbol, not an asterisk. Words.

  2. In the article

    A bordered disclosure box near the top of any article or recipe that contains affiliate links, above the first one.

  3. On this page

    The full policy, linked from the footer of every page on the site.

Our rules for affiliate links

  • We only link to equipment and ingredients we have actually used in the Limassol kitchen.
  • We never write a recipe in order to sell a product.
  • Where a cheaper option, or no purchase at all, would do the job, we say so. Several of our recipes explicitly tell you not to buy anything.
  • We do not accept payment to include a particular retailer.
  • Affiliate links carry rel="sponsored nofollow" so search engines can see the relationship too.
  • Where we link to a Cypriot or EU retailer, you keep the full EU consumer rights against them, including the 14-day right of withdrawal on a distance purchase. Those rights are with the retailer, not with us.

5. Sponsored and brand-partnered recipes

Sometimes a brand pays us to develop a recipe using their product. When that happens:

  • The page carries a Sponsored recipe label in a bordered box at the very top of the page, above the headline and above the recipe. Not in the footer, not in a hashtag, not in fine print at the end. The brand that paid is named in that label.
  • We develop, cook, test and photograph the recipe ourselves, to the same standard as everything else on the site.
  • The brand may correct factual errors about their own product. They do not write the method and they do not control our opinion.
  • We do not accept payment for a positive review or for a specific rating.
  • We do not make health, nutrition, weight-loss or therapeutic claims about any product. Nutrition and health claims on food are restricted in the EU by Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 to claims on the authorised list, and we are not in the business of making them.
  • If a product turns out not to work in testing, we tell the brand, refund or renegotiate, and do not publish.

You can see the labelling in practice on our roast pumpkin, chickpea and rocket salad.

6. Gifted products

Brands sometimes send us equipment or ingredients without payment. We do not promise coverage in exchange, and most gifted items are never mentioned on the site. If we do write about something that was gifted, we say so in the article, whether or not any money changed hands. A free pan is a benefit, and a benefit is a commercial relationship even when no invoice exists.

7. Paid digital products

We sell downloadable cookbooks and seasonal meal-plan bundles, priced in euros and including VAT.

  • Seasonal meal-plan bundles from €9.00
  • Full year meal-plan bundle €15.00

Prices shown are the total you pay, inclusive of tax, with no charge added at the last step. You have a 14-day right of withdrawal on any of these under EU consumer law, set out in clause 7 of our Terms of Use.

These are optional. Nothing that is free on the site is ever moved behind a purchase, and the weekly meal plans on the meal plans page stay free.

8. Membership

Meraki Table Membership is €4.50 a month. It provides ad-free browsing, printable recipe cards and saved collections that sync across devices.

It is a rolling monthly subscription, cancellable at any time in two clicks, with no minimum term and no cancellation fee. Membership removes advertising and adds convenience. It does not unlock recipes. Every recipe on this site is available to everyone, free, whether or not you are a member, and that is not going to change.

9. Newsletter sponsorship

The Thursday newsletter sometimes carries a single sponsor. The placement is labelled inside the email, above the sponsored content, and the sponsor is named. There is never more than one sponsor per send, and sponsored placements are never written to look like our own editorial.

Sponsors never receive subscriber email addresses. They pay for a placement, not for the list. We could not sell it even if we wanted to: your address is processed on the basis of your consent for our newsletter and nothing else. Details are on the work with us page.

10. What we will not do

  • Publish a recipe we have not cooked and tested.
  • Present advertising as editorial content.
  • Make health, medical, nutritional, weight-loss or therapeutic claims about food or products.
  • Publish fabricated reviews, fake star ratings or invented reader testimonials. Presenting a paid or invented review as genuine is on the EU blacklist of commercial practices that are unfair in all circumstances.
  • Use unsubstantiated superlatives such as best, number one, guaranteed or miracle.
  • Create false urgency, countdown timers or fake scarcity.
  • Sell, rent or share reader data with advertisers.
  • Promote gambling, weight-loss products, supplements or infant formula.

11. Why we publish all of this

Two reasons. The first is legal: EU and Cypriot consumer law require that commercial relationships are not hidden from readers, and the Consumer Protection Service enforces that in Cyprus.

The second is simpler. If you are going to trust a recipe enough to spend money and an hour of your evening on it, you are entitled to know whether the person who wrote it was paid to recommend the pan.

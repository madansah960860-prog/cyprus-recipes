Privacy Policy

What personal data we collect, why we are allowed to process it, how long we keep it, and the rights you hold over it. Written to comply with the General Data Protection Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2016/679, and the Cyprus Law providing for the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data, Law 125(I)/2018.

Last updated 14 September 2026. Version 2.0.

Please note. This policy is a starting template and must be reviewed by a Cyprus-qualified lawyer before this site goes live. Do not rely on it as legal advice.

1. Who we are, and who is responsible for your data

This site is published by Meraki Table Media Ltd, Reg. No. HE 412356 · VAT CY10412356X, of 24 Anexartisias Street, Office 3, Limassol 3040, Cyprus. In this policy, we, us and our mean Meraki Table Media Ltd.

We are the data controller for the personal data described here. That means we decide why your data is processed and how, and we carry the responsibility for it under the GDPR.

You can contact us about data protection at privacy@merakitable.com.cy, by post at the address above, or by phone on +357 25 000 000.

We are a small publisher and our processing is limited, so we have not appointed a Data Protection Officer under Article 37 of the GDPR. Data protection questions are handled by the address above and answered by a person.

Personal data, in this policy, has the meaning the GDPR gives it: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. An email address is personal data. So, in most circumstances, is an IP address.

2. What personal data we collect

We collect only what we need to run the site and provide what you have asked for. There is no account required to read a recipe.

Data you give us directly

  • Contact form. Your name, email address, chosen topic and the content of your message.
  • Newsletter. Your email address, your first name if you give it, your chosen send frequency, and a record of the date, time and page at which you gave consent. We keep that consent record because Article 7(1) of the GDPR requires a controller to be able to demonstrate that consent was given.
  • Purchases. If you buy a cookbook, a meal-plan bundle or a membership, we collect your name, email address, billing address and country for VAT purposes. Card details go straight to our payment processor and are never stored on our servers.
  • Correspondence. Anything you choose to tell us by email, by post or on the telephone.

Data collected automatically

  • Server logs. Your IP address, browser type and version, device type, operating system, the page you requested, the referring page and the date and time of the request. These are recorded by our hosting provider for security and troubleshooting.
  • Analytics. If you consent to analytics cookies, aggregated information about which pages are visited, how long people stay and how they arrived.
  • Advertising. If you consent to advertising cookies, our advertising partners including Google may collect information about your browsing in order to select and measure advertisements.

Data stored on your own device, not sent to us

Some features store information in your browser rather than sending it anywhere. Ticked ingredient checkboxes exist only while the page is open. Saved recipes and your cookie preference are kept in your browser's local storage on your device. We cannot read any of it, and clearing your browser data removes it.

Special category data

We do not seek and do not want special category data as defined in Article 9 of the GDPR, which includes health data, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetic and biometric data, sex life and sexual orientation. Please do not send it to us. If you mention a food allergy in a message so that we can answer your question, we use it for that reply only and delete it with the rest of the correspondence.

4. Cookies and similar technologies

A cookie is a small text file placed on your device by a website. Cookies and similar storage are governed in Cyprus by the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC as implemented in national law, which requires your consent before any cookie that is not strictly necessary is placed. We use three categories.

  • Essential cookies and storage are strictly necessary for the site to work, including remembering the cookie choice you made. These do not require consent.
  • Analytics cookies help us understand, in aggregate, how the site is used. Consent only.
  • Advertising cookies are set by third parties including Google to select and measure advertising. Consent only.

No non-essential cookie is set before you choose. On your first visit a banner offers Accept all, Reject non-essential and Manage preferences, with rejection as easy and as prominent as acceptance. Nothing optional loads until you have decided. You can change your mind at any time through the Cookie settings link in the footer of every page, and withdrawal takes effect immediately for future processing.

Google and advertising cookies

Where you consent to advertising cookies, third party vendors including Google may use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this and other websites. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visit to our site and other sites on the internet. You can opt out of personalised advertising through Google's Ads Settings, and you can opt out of some third party vendors' use of cookies for personalised advertising through the industry opt-out pages operated by the Digital Advertising Alliance and the Network Advertising Initiative. Withdrawing consent here removes it for the future; it does not undo processing that already, lawfully, took place.

Full detail, including a list of what we set, is in our Cookie Policy.

5. Who we share personal data with

We do not sell personal data. We do not rent or trade mailing lists. We share personal data only with the following categories of recipient, only as far as necessary, and, where they process on our behalf, only under a written processor agreement meeting Article 28 of the GDPR.

  • Hosting and infrastructure providers, who store the site and its logs. Processors.
  • Email service providers, who send the newsletter and hold the subscriber list. Processors.
  • Payment processors, who handle purchases and receive your billing details directly. Independent controllers for their own fraud and regulatory duties.
  • Analytics providers, where you have consented to analytics cookies. Processors.
  • Advertising partners, including Google, where you have consented to advertising cookies. Generally independent or joint controllers for the advertising they select, under their own published terms.
  • Professional advisers such as accountants and lawyers, where necessary and under a duty of confidence.
  • Courts, law enforcement or regulators, where we are required by law to disclose.

Sponsors and advertisers never receive your email address or any other contact detail from us.

6. Transfers outside the EEA

We prefer providers that keep data inside the European Economic Area, and several of ours do. Some do not. Depending on the provider, your data may be processed in the United States or in other countries outside the EEA where those providers operate data centres.

Chapter V of the GDPR allows such a transfer only where it is properly safeguarded. Where a transfer outside the EEA happens, we rely on one of the following, in this order of preference:

  • an adequacy decision of the European Commission under Article 45, where one covers the destination country or framework;
  • otherwise, the Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by the European Commission under Article 46(2)(c), incorporated into our contract with that provider, together with a transfer impact assessment and any supplementary technical measures the assessment calls for, such as encryption in transit and at rest.

You are entitled to a copy of the safeguards we rely on for any particular transfer. Ask at privacy@merakitable.com.cy and we will send them, with commercial terms redacted.

7. How we store and secure personal data

Personal data is held on servers operated by our hosting and service providers. As Article 32 of the GDPR requires, we apply technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, including:

  • serving the entire site over HTTPS;
  • restricting access to personal data to the people who need it to do their job;
  • multi-factor authentication on every administrative account;
  • keeping software and dependencies patched;
  • encryption of data at rest with our main providers;
  • data minimisation, which is the most effective measure of all: we collect as little as we can.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot promise absolute security.

If something goes wrong

If a personal data breach occurs, we will notify the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection of the Republic of Cyprus without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, as Article 33 of the GDPR requires, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms. Where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to you, we will also tell you directly and in plain language, as Article 34 requires. We keep an internal record of every breach, including those we do not have to report.

9. Your rights

The GDPR gives you the following rights over your personal data. They are free to exercise, and asking will never affect how we treat you.

  • Access (Article 15). To be told whether we hold data about you and, if so, to receive a copy of it along with the purposes, the recipients, the retention period and the source.
  • Rectification (Article 16). To have inaccurate data corrected and incomplete data completed, including by giving us a supplementary statement.
  • Erasure (Article 17). To have your data deleted where it is no longer needed, where you withdraw the consent it rested on, where you successfully object, or where it has been processed unlawfully. This is not absolute: we may keep what a legal obligation requires us to keep, such as an invoice.
  • Restriction (Article 18). To have processing paused rather than deleted, for example while we check a correction you have asked for.
  • Portability (Article 20). Where processing is based on your consent or on a contract and is carried out by automated means, to receive the data you gave us in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and to have it sent directly to another controller where that is technically feasible.
  • Objection (Article 21). To object at any time to processing based on our legitimate interests, on grounds relating to your situation. We then stop unless we can show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, or the processing is for a legal claim. Where you object to direct marketing, there is no balancing at all: we stop, immediately and permanently.
  • Not to be subject to automated decisions (Article 22). As set out in section 3, we do not make such decisions.
  • Withdrawal of consent (Article 7(3)). Where processing rests on your consent, you may withdraw it at any time, and it must be as easy to withdraw as it was to give. Use the unsubscribe link in any newsletter, the Cookie settings link in the footer, or email us. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before you withdrew.

How to exercise them

Email privacy@merakitable.com.cy or write to the postal address above, and tell us which right you are exercising and what data you mean, so we can find it. We may need to confirm your identity before we release anything, which usually means nothing more than replying from the address we already hold.

We respond within one month, as Article 12(3) requires. Where a request is particularly complex, or where you have made several, we may extend that by up to two further months, and we will tell you within the first month that we are doing so and why. Requests are free. In the rare case where a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive, particularly a repetitive one, we may charge a reasonable administrative fee or refuse it, and we will explain our reasoning and your right to complain before doing either. If we refuse a request, we will tell you why, in writing, within one month.

10. Unsubscribing from email

Every marketing email we send carries a working unsubscribe link. One click removes you, at no cost, and takes effect immediately. You can also reply to any email and ask us to stop, or email privacy@merakitable.com.cy. Unsubscribing withdraws your consent under Article 7(3) and is also an objection to direct marketing under Article 21(2), which we act on without argument. We keep only a minimal record that you unsubscribed, so that we do not accidentally add you again.

11. Using the site without identifying yourself

You can read every recipe on this site without telling us anything about yourself. There is no account, no wall and no sign-in. We ask for identifying data only when you want a reply, subscribe to the newsletter, or buy something. That is data minimisation under Article 5(1)(c), and it is also just good manners.

12. Children

This site is written for a general adult audience. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. In Cyprus the age at which a child can consent to information society services under Article 8 of the GDPR is 14, and we do not offer the newsletter or any purchase to anyone under that age. If you believe a child has given us personal data, contact us and we will erase it.

13. Links to other sites

The site links to other websites, including retailers through affiliate links. Once you leave us, their privacy policies apply and ours does not. We are not responsible for their practices or their content, and we suggest you read what they say before handing anything over.

14. How to complain

If you think we have handled your personal data badly or breached the GDPR, please tell us first. Email privacy@merakitable.com.cy with the word Complaint in the subject line, or write to the postal address above, and describe what happened. We acknowledge within 5 working days and give you a written response within one month.

You also have the right, under Article 77 of the GDPR, to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority whether or not you come to us first. The supervisory authority for Cyprus is the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection of the Republic of Cyprus, at 15 Kypranoros Street, 1061 Nicosia, Cyprus, telephone +357 22 818 456, email commissioner@dataprotection.gov.cy.

If you live or work in another EU member state, or the alleged infringement happened there, you may instead complain to the supervisory authority of that member state, and it will coordinate with the Cyprus Commissioner. You also have the right to an effective judicial remedy under Articles 78 and 79.

15. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy. The current version and its date are always shown at the top of this page. Where a change is significant we will say so on the site, and where it affects data you have already given us we will notify subscribers by email before it takes effect. Where a change means we need a fresh consent, we will ask for it rather than assume it.

Cookie Policy

What we store on your device, why, and how to change it. Under EU law nothing optional may be stored before you agree, so nothing optional is. Rejecting costs you nothing: the whole site works either way.

Last updated 14 September 2026. Version 2.0.

Please note. This legal copy is a starting template and must be reviewed by a Cyprus-qualified lawyer before launch. It is not legal advice.

1. What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. It lets a site remember something between page loads, such as whether you have already made a choice about cookies. Related technologies do similar jobs: local storage keeps information in your browser without ever sending it to a server, and pixels are tiny image requests used to record that a page was viewed.

EU law treats all of these the same way. It is not about the word cookie, it is about storing information on, or reading information from, your device. This policy covers the lot, and forms part of our Privacy Policy.

2. The rule we work to

Storage on your device is governed in Cyprus by the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC as implemented in Cypriot law, which permits only cookies that are strictly necessary to deliver the service you asked for without your consent. Everything else requires your prior, informed, freely given consent. Where a cookie also involves personal data, the GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679, and the Cyprus Law providing for the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data, Law 125(I)/2018, apply to that data as well, and our lawful basis for the non-essential categories is your consent under Article 6(1)(a).

In practice that means:

  • On your first visit you see a banner with three choices: Accept all, Reject non-essential and Manage preferences.
  • No analytics or advertising cookie is set before you choose. Nothing optional loads while the banner is sitting there. Silence is not consent, continuing to scroll is not consent, and a pre-ticked box is not consent.
  • Rejecting is one click, in the same place, in the same size button as accepting. Refusing must be as easy as agreeing, so it is.
  • Consent is given per category. You can accept analytics and refuse advertising, or the reverse.
  • You can change your mind at any time through the Cookie settings link in the footer of every page. Withdrawal must be as easy as consent, and it takes effect immediately for anything that happens afterwards.
  • Rejecting non-essential cookies restricts nothing. Every recipe, tool, filter and page works exactly the same.

We also record what you chose and when, because Article 7(1) of the GDPR requires us to be able to demonstrate that consent was given.

3. Essential storage, no consent required

These are strictly necessary for the site to function and are exempt from the consent requirement. They carry no advertising or tracking identifier, and they never leave your device.

4. Analytics cookies, consent only

If you accept analytics, we use them to understand in aggregate which recipes people actually use, how long they stay and where they came from. That tells us what to cook next. It is not used to identify you, and analytics data is not shared with advertisers.

If you refuse, we simply know less, and the site behaves identically.

5. Advertising cookies, consent only

Free recipes on this site are paid for by advertising. If you accept advertising cookies, third party vendors including Google may set cookies on your device to select advertisements and to measure whether they were seen.

Google specifically

  • Third party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this website and other websites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visit to our site and other sites on the internet.
  • For the advertising it selects, Google acts as a controller in its own right under its own published terms, alongside us. It is not simply processing on our instructions.
  • You may opt out of personalised advertising through Google's Ads Settings page.
  • You may opt out of some third party vendors' use of cookies for personalised advertising through the industry opt-out pages run by the Digital Advertising Alliance and the Network Advertising Initiative.
  • Withdrawing consent here stops future use. It does not make lawful past processing unlawful, and it does not by itself erase data a partner already holds. To have that erased, exercise your rights with the partner directly, or ask us and we will pass the request on.

If you reject advertising cookies, you will still see advertising on the site. It will simply be selected without using your browsing history, which usually means it is less relevant to you and worth less to us. That is a trade we are happy for you to make.

6. Third party embedded content

We keep embedded third party content to a minimum. The site loads web fonts and photographs from third party services in order to display the pages at all. Those requests set no advertising cookie, but the provider will receive your IP address, as happens with any resource loaded from another domain. That is an unavoidable part of how the web works rather than a tracking choice on our part.

We embed no social media widgets, no comment systems and no video players that follow you across sites.

7. Changing or withdrawing your consent

  1. On this site

    Select Cookie settings in the footer of any page. The banner reopens with the preference panel expanded and your current choice shown. Change it and save. This is the quickest route and it is available on every single page.

  2. In your browser

    All major browsers let you block or delete cookies for one site or for every site. Look under Settings, then Privacy. Blocking essential storage means the banner will reappear on every visit, because we can no longer remember what you told us.

  3. Global Privacy Control

    Where your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a refusal of all non-essential cookies, without waiting for you to interact with the banner.

We ask again, from scratch, if we add a new category of cookie, if a partner changes materially, and in any event no more than once every twelve months. Consent is not something we treat as permanent.

8. Your rights over data collected through cookies

Where a cookie involves personal data, you hold the full set of GDPR rights over it: access under Article 15, rectification under Article 16, erasure under Article 17, restriction under Article 18, portability under Article 20, objection under Article 21, and withdrawal of consent under Article 7(3). How to exercise each is set out in section 9 of the Privacy Policy.

If you are unhappy with how we handle any of it, you can complain to the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection of the Republic of Cyprus, which supervises both the GDPR and the Cypriot implementation of the ePrivacy Directive. If you live or work in another EU member state, you may complain to the supervisory authority there instead. Contact details are in the Privacy Policy.

9. Changes to this policy

If we add or remove a category of cookie, we update this page and the date at the top, and where the change is material we ask for consent again rather than relying on the consent you gave for something else.

Accessibility Statement

This site is built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA. Here is what that means in practice, what we know is imperfect, and how to tell us when something does not work.

Last updated 14 September 2026. Version 2.0.

Please note. This statement is a starting template and must be reviewed by a Cyprus-qualified lawyer before launch, alongside a full independent accessibility audit. It is not legal advice.

1. Our commitment

A recipe is useless if you cannot read it. We want this site to work for people using screen readers, keyboard navigation, screen magnification, voice control, high contrast modes and reduced motion settings, on a phone in a kitchen with wet hands as much as on a desktop.

Our target is WCAG 2.2 Level AA, the current version of the standard published by the W3C.

We are a private publisher, so the obligations that bind public sector bodies do not bind us. We have chosen to aim at the same standards anyway, because they are the right ones and because the direction of EU law is clear:

  • the Web Accessibility Directive, Directive (EU) 2016/2102, which requires public sector websites and mobile applications across the Union to be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust, and which is met in practice by the harmonised European standard EN 301 549;
  • the European Accessibility Act, Directive (EU) 2019/882, which extends accessibility requirements to a range of private sector products and services, including e-commerce, and which is transposed in Cyprus as in every member state.

Where we sell a cookbook or a membership, that is e-commerce, and we build and test it to the same standard as the free recipes around it.

2. What we have done

Structure and navigation

  • Semantic HTML5 landmarks on every page: header, nav, main and footer, so assistive technology can jump between regions.
  • A skip to content link as the first focusable element on every page.
  • A single, logical heading order on each page, starting with one h1.
  • Breadcrumbs on inner pages, marked up as a labelled navigation list.
  • The current page is marked in the navigation with aria-current="page".
  • Every page is in English and declares it, so a screen reader pronounces the Greek loanwords in these recipes with a consistent voice rather than switching mid-sentence.

Keyboard and focus

  • Every interactive element can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone, including the mobile menu, the filters, the accordions, the servings stepper and the unit toggle.
  • A clearly visible focus outline on every focusable element, never obscured by a sticky header or a cookie banner. We have not removed focus styling anywhere.
  • The mobile drawer traps focus while open, closes on Escape, and returns focus to the button that opened it.
  • No keyboard traps anywhere on the site.
  • No drag-only interaction: anything you can drag, you can also do with a single click or tap.

Content and images

  • Descriptive alternative text on every meaningful image, describing the food rather than repeating the recipe title.
  • Decorative elements are hidden from assistive technology with aria-hidden.
  • Width and height on every image, so the page does not shift as images load.
  • Link text that makes sense out of context. No bare click here.
  • Data tables use proper headers and captions.

Colour, contrast and text

  • Body text meets or exceeds a 4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background, and large text meets 3:1.
  • Interface components and focus indicators meet 3:1 against what is next to them.
  • Colour is never the only way information is conveyed. Filters that are active are also marked with aria-pressed, and ticked ingredients are struck through as well as coloured.
  • Text resizes to 200 per cent without loss of content or function, and the layout reflows at 320 pixels wide.
  • Body text is set at 17 pixels with a 1.7 line height, and line length is capped for readability.

Forms

  • Every field has a visible label. We do not use placeholder text as a label.
  • Errors appear next to the field they relate to, in text as well as colour, and are announced to assistive technology.
  • Required fields are marked, and the first field with an error receives focus on submit.
  • Success messages are announced through a live region rather than only appearing visually.
  • Nothing on this site asks you to remember or retype information you have already given it in the same process, and no step depends on solving a puzzle or recognising an image.

Motion

  • Animation is limited to short fades, a small image scale on hover and smooth accordion transitions.
  • All of it is disabled when your system requests reduced motion, through prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Nothing moves, flashes or auto-plays on load. There is no parallax, no carousel that advances itself and no video.

Things we deliberately do not do

  • We do not disable the browser back button, block right-click, or interfere with zoom.
  • We do not open pop-ups, pop-unders or entry interstitials.
  • We do not hide content behind an overlay on page load.
  • We do not trigger automatic downloads or automatic redirects.
  • We do not use an accessibility overlay widget. They tend to paper over the problem rather than fix it, and we would rather fix it.

3. Known limitations

We would rather list these than pretend they do not exist.

  • Advertising. Third party advertising is served by external partners and we do not control its markup. Some ad creatives may not meet the standard we hold our own content to. Members browsing ad-free do not see them at all.
  • Third party photography. Images are served from an external image host. If that service is unavailable, alternative text is displayed, but the page will look different.
  • Long collection pages. Pages that carry several complete recipes are long. We have added in-page anchors and a jump link, but they are still a lot of content to move through with a screen reader.
  • Greek and Cypriot terms. Words such as koupepia, afelia, kolokasi and pourgouri are given in Latin script with a short gloss on first use. A screen reader will pronounce them as English, which is imperfect, and we have not marked each one up with a language change because switching voice mid-sentence tested worse than leaving it alone.
  • Independent audit. This statement is based on our own testing. A full independent accessibility audit has not yet been completed.

4. How we test

  • Keyboard-only navigation through every page and every interactive component.
  • Screen reader testing with the tools built into the operating systems we have access to.
  • Automated checks for contrast, heading order, landmarks, alt text and form labelling.
  • Manual checks at 320, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 pixels wide, and at 200 per cent text zoom.
  • Testing with reduced motion and increased contrast settings enabled.
  • A check of every new recipe page before it publishes, against the same list.

5. Tell us when something does not work

If any part of this site is difficult or impossible for you to use, please tell us. Real reports find problems that testing misses, and we take them seriously.

Email access@merakitable.com.cy, use the contact form and choose the accessibility topic, or write to Meraki Table Media Ltd, Reg. No. HE 412356 · VAT CY10412356X, 24 Anexartisias Street, Office 3, Limassol 3040, Cyprus. You can also telephone +357 25 000 000.

Please tell us the page address, what you were trying to do, and what happened. If you can, mention the browser and any assistive technology you were using, because it helps us reproduce it. Write in whatever form suits you: a rough note is worth far more to us than no note.

What happens next

  1. We acknowledge within 2 business days

    A person replies, not an automated ticket.

  2. We aim to fix within 10 business days

    For anything that blocks access to a recipe, sooner.

  3. We tell you when it is done

    And if we cannot fix something, we explain why and describe the alternative.

6. If you are not satisfied

If we do not resolve your complaint, tell us again and ask for it to be looked at by someone else here. If that still does not settle it, you may raise the matter with the Commissioner for Administration and the Protection of Human Rights of the Republic of Cyprus, the Ombudsman, who acts as the national equality body and handles complaints of discrimination on grounds of disability under Cypriot and EU law.

Complaining costs you nothing and does not affect how we deal with you.

7. Alternative ways to get a recipe

If the site itself is not working for you, we will send you the recipe in a plain text email or a plain document, at no cost. Email access@merakitable.com.cy with the recipe name and we will send it the same week. If a large print version or a simpler layout would help, ask for that instead and we will make one.

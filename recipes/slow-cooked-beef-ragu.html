Slow-Cooked Beef Ragù with Pasta

By Elena Christodoulou. Published 29/07/2026. Tested five times, twice in a clay pot and three times in a cast iron casserole.

Cyprus has always cooked meat slowly and left it alone: afelia in its wine and coriander, kleftiko sealed into a wood oven, a pot of stifado pushed to the back of the stove. This ragu belongs to the same habit. Three hours in the oven, of which about twenty minutes involve you, chuck steak in whole pieces so it shreds into long strands, and a parmesan rind doing quiet work the entire time.

Chuck steak, not mince

Mince makes a bolognese. Whole pieces of chuck, braised until they collapse, make a ragù with texture, and chuck is one of the cheapest cuts at any Cypriot butcher. Ask for a 1.2 kg piece and cut it into 5 cm chunks yourself, or buy it ready diced and accept slightly smaller pieces. Shin and beef cheek both work if that is what looks good on the day, and a butcher in Limassol or Nicosia will usually push you towards whichever is best that week.

The browning stage is where most of the flavour is made, and it is the stage people rush. Work in three batches with plenty of space, and let each piece sit undisturbed until it releases from the pan. If the pan is crowded the beef will grey rather than brown, and the finished sauce will taste thin no matter how long it cooks.

Keep the parmesan rinds from wedges you have finished. They live in the freezer and go straight into braises and soups. It is the least fashionable ingredient in this recipe and the one that makes the biggest difference. A rind of dry anari or a hard kefalotyri crust does a similar job if that is what is in your fridge.

Serve it over pourgouri

Pasta is the obvious bed and not the only one. Cracked wheat, pourgouri, toasted first in oil with a little vermicelli and finely chopped onion, then simmered in stock until the grains stand apart, takes a shredded meat sauce better than almost anything. It is what many Cypriot households would put under a braise like this without thinking about it, alongside a bowl of plain yoghurt and a few slices of raw onion. Plain boiled potatoes and village bread for the plate are the third option, and no worse.

Method

  1. Heat the oven and season the beef

    Heat the oven to 160°C, or 140°C fan-forced. Pat the beef dry and season it well with salt. Dry meat browns, wet meat steams.

  2. Brown in three batches

    Heat half the oil in a heavy casserole over high heat. Brown the beef in three batches, about 4 minutes a side, until each piece has a genuine crust. Do not crowd the pan. Set the browned beef aside on a plate and keep any juices.

  3. Cook the soffritto slowly

    Drop the heat to medium, add the remaining oil, then the onion, carrot and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook for 12 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and sweet with no raw edge. This is not a stage to hurry.

  4. Bloom the paste

    Add the garlic and tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until the paste darkens from bright red to brick. This removes the tinny taste.

  5. Deglaze

    Pour in the red wine and scrape the base of the pot with a wooden spoon to lift everything stuck to it. Simmer until reduced by half, about 4 minutes.

  6. Build the braise

    Return the beef and its juices. Add the crushed tomatoes, stock, bay leaves, parmesan rind and salt. Bring to a bare simmer, then put the lid on.

  7. Three hours in the oven

    Transfer to the oven and cook for 3 hours. Check once at the two hour mark, and add a splash of water if the surface looks dry. The beef should fall apart under light pressure from a spoon.

  8. Shred and season

    Fish out the bay leaves and the parmesan rind. Shred the beef in the sauce with two forks. Taste and adjust the salt now, not before, because the sauce has concentrated.

  9. Finish the pasta in the sauce

    Cook the pasta in well salted water until 2 minutes short of the packet time. Lift it straight into the sauce with a ladle of pasta water, add the grated parmesan and toss over medium heat for 2 minutes until the sauce clings. Scatter with parsley and serve.

Cook's tips

  • Salt the sauce at the end, not the start. Three hours of reduction concentrates everything. A ragù seasoned perfectly at the beginning will taste too salty by the time it is done.
  • Keep a cup of pasta water. The starch in it is what turns sauce and pasta into one thing instead of two. Add it a splash at a time while tossing.
  • Cook it a day ahead if you can. This tastes noticeably better on day two. The fat sets on top overnight, which also makes it easy to remove if you want a lighter sauce.
  • A cinnamon stick and a strip of orange peel in the pot for the last hour push the sauce towards a Cypriot stifado. Two cloves as well, if you grew up with that.
  • The wine is not essential. If you do not cook with alcohol, replace it with 250 ml of stock plus 2 teaspoons of red wine vinegar added at the end for lift.

Storage

Refrigerate the sauce, without pasta, in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water. Do not store cooked pasta in the sauce, it keeps absorbing liquid and goes soft.

Freeze in portions for up to 3 months. Flat containers or zip bags laid flat thaw fastest. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat to steaming hot.

Make ahead

The whole sauce can be made up to 2 days ahead, which is the way we usually cook it: Saturday afternoon while the house is quiet, Sunday lunch with people around the table. The soffritto alone can be made and frozen in ice cube trays, which turns this into a much shorter job next time.

Slow cooker version

Brown the beef and make the soffritto on the stove as written, then transfer everything to a slow cooker. Cook on low for 8 hours or high for 5. Because a slow cooker does not evaporate liquid, reduce the stock to 125 ml, and if the finished sauce is loose, simmer it uncovered on the stove for 10 minutes.

Allergens

Contains gluten (pasta) and dairy (parmesan). Contains sulphites if you use wine. For a gluten free version use a good quality gluten free pasta and finish it in the sauce for one minute rather than two, since it softens faster. Always read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Questions people ask about this recipe

Chuck carries a fair amount of fat, and three hours renders all of it. Let the pot sit for ten minutes off the heat and spoon the clear fat off the surface, or refrigerate overnight and lift the set fat off in one piece. Keeping a little back is good, it carries flavour.

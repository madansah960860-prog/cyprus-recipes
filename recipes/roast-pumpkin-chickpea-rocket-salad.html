Roast Pumpkin, Chickpea and Rocket Salad

By Elena Christodoulou. Published 05/08/2026. Tested twice, once with anari and once with grilled halloumi.

Chickpeas, rocket, tahini and lemon are the four corners of a Cypriot kitchen, which is why this reads as a local salad rather than a borrowed one. The chickpeas crisp on the same tray as the pumpkin, the rocket wilts a little under the hot vegetables, and the dressing keeps a week in the fridge. Put anari or a slab of grilled halloumi on top and it is dinner.

Buy the pumpkin in autumn and leave the skin on

Cyprus pumpkins come in from late September and stack up at the laiki through October and November, sold whole or cut into wedges by weight. Ask for a dense, dry-fleshed one rather than a watery kolokotha; the vendor will know what you mean and will usually let you see the cut face. The skin on a local pumpkin softens enough to eat. Butternut works too but goes watery if the oven is not hot enough, and any thick-skinned blue variety should be peeled.

Skin on is not laziness. It holds the wedge together on the tray, it browns beautifully, and it adds fibre. Scrub the pumpkin under the tap, dry it, and cut it into wedges about 3 cm at the thickest point.

The chickpeas need to be properly dry before they go in the oven. Drain the tin, tip them onto a clean tea towel, and roll them around for thirty seconds. Wet chickpeas steam and stay soft, which is fine but is not what this salad wants.

Halloumi, anari, or neither

Anari is the fresh whey cheese made when halloumi is made, mild and milky, and crumbled over hot vegetables it behaves much like a young feta. Fresh anari from a village dairy is the better version; the dried, salted anari sold for grating is a different thing and should be grated, not crumbled. If you want the salad to carry a whole dinner, cut halloumi into 1 cm slabs, dry-fry it until it blisters on both sides, and lay it over the top at the last moment so it is still squeaking. Rocket, tahini and halloumi have been sitting on the same table here for a very long time. This is not a fusion.

Method

  1. Heat the oven

    Heat the oven to 220°C, or 200°C fan-forced. Line the largest tray you own with baking paper. A crowded tray steams, so use two trays rather than piling everything onto one.

  2. Season the tray

    Toss the pumpkin wedges and the dried chickpeas with the olive oil, cumin, smoked paprika and salt flakes. Spread them out so nothing overlaps, with the pumpkin cut side down.

  3. Roast

    Roast for 30 minutes, turning the pumpkin once at the 20 minute mark. You are looking for dark caramelised edges on the pumpkin and chickpeas that rattle slightly when you shake the tray.

  4. Make the dressing

    Whisk the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and honey in a bowl. It will seize and look ruined. Keep whisking and add the cold water a tablespoon at a time until it loosens into a pourable cream. This always happens and it always comes back.

  5. Soften the onion

    Toss the sliced red onion with a squeeze of extra lemon juice and a pinch of salt. Leave it for 10 minutes while the tray finishes. It turns pink and loses its raw bite.

  6. Assemble while warm

    Spread the rocket over a large platter. Tip the hot pumpkin and chickpeas straight on top so the leaves wilt a little underneath. Scatter with the anari or feta, the pepitas and the pink onion.

  7. Dress and serve

    Spoon the tahini dressing over in ribbons rather than tossing it through, so you get pockets of leaf, dressing and pumpkin instead of a uniform beige salad. Serve immediately with the extra dressing on the table.

Cook's tips

  • The dressing will seize. Do not panic. Tahini stiffens the moment acid hits it, then relaxes as you add water. Cold water works better than warm, and adding it slowly gives a glossier result.
  • Dry the chickpeas like you mean it. Thirty seconds in a tea towel is the difference between crisp chickpeas and soft ones. Any skins that come loose can go in the bin, they burn first.
  • Dress at the table if it needs to travel. Take the tahini in a jar and the salad undressed. This is what makes it a good dish to bring to someone else's house.
  • Save the pumpkin seeds. Rinse the seeds from the middle, dry them, toss with oil and salt, and roast them alongside for 12 minutes. Free pepitas, and nobody in a Cypriot household throws out a pumpkin seed.
  • Add pomegranate in October. A handful of seeds scattered at the end gives sharp bursts against the tahini, and the trees are dropping fruit all over the island by then.

Storage

Undressed components keep separately in the fridge for 3 days. Store the roast pumpkin and chickpeas in one container and the dressing in a jar. Assembled salad does not keep well because the rocket collapses, so build only what you plan to eat.

The tahini dressing lasts a week in the fridge. It will thicken as it sits, so loosen it with a spoonful of water before using.

Make ahead

Roast the pumpkin and chickpeas in the morning and leave them at room temperature. Warm them for 8 minutes at 180°C, or 160°C fan-forced, before assembling. The dressing can be made 5 days ahead and the pepitas toasted a week ahead.

Make it vegan

Leave out the anari or feta and use carob syrup or maple syrup instead of honey. To replace what the cheese was doing, add 2 tablespoons of capers, rinsed and fried in a little oil until they burst open, and an extra pinch of salt flakes. Cyprus capers sold on the stem are excellent here. Nothing else changes.

Allergens

Contains sesame (tahini) and dairy (anari, feta or halloumi). The vegan version contains sesame only. Chickpeas are a legume, which some people react to. Always read the packaging on the products in your own kitchen and speak to a health professional about any dietary or medical need.

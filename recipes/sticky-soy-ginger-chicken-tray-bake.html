Sticky Soy and Ginger Chicken Tray Bake

By Elena Christodoulou. Published 19/08/2026. Tested three times, all of them on an actual Tuesday.

One tray, one mixing bowl, about fifteen minutes of real work. Nothing about this is Cypriot except the potatoes, and that is the point: it is what gets cooked on a weeknight when the shopping was short and nobody wants to stand at the stove. The glaze goes on late so the honey caramelises instead of burning, and the potatoes get a head start so everything finishes together.

Why this one works on a Tuesday

Most tray bakes fail in one of two ways. Either the sauce goes on at the start and turns to bitter black lacquer, or everything goes in at once and the potatoes are still hard when the chicken is dry. This version fixes both by staging the tray: potatoes and onion first on a preheated tray, chicken and peppers next, glaze only in the last twelve minutes.

Thigh fillets are the right cut here. Breast will overcook in the time it takes the potatoes to soften, and thigh has enough fat to sit happily in a sticky sauce. If you can find skin-on thigh at the butcher, use it and lay the pieces skin up. Alphamega and Lidl Cyprus mostly sell skinless thigh fillets, which work fine.

Use Cyprus potatoes and do not peel them. The small red-earth ones from the Kokkinochoria dig up dense and waxy, they hold their shape through half an hour at 220°C, and the skin crisps instead of slumping. They are one of the few things on this island that need no argument in their favour.

The shopping is short: chicken, potatoes, two peppers, and a handful of things already in the cupboard. Soy, rice vinegar and sesame oil are in the international aisle at Alphamega and Sklavenitis, and cheaper at any Asian grocer in Limassol or Nicosia. The whole tray goes on the table and there is one dish to wash.

Method

  1. Heat the oven and the tray

    Heat the oven to 220°C, or 200°C fan-forced. Slide a large heavy roasting tray onto the middle shelf while the oven comes up to temperature. Putting food onto an already hot tray is the single biggest difference between pale potatoes and crisp ones.

  2. Dry the chicken

    Pat the thigh fillets completely dry with paper towel and leave them on a plate at room temperature while the oven heats. Surface moisture turns to steam, and steam stops browning.

  3. Start the potatoes

    Toss the potatoes and onion wedges with half the oil and the salt. Carefully tip them onto the hot tray, cut side down, and roast for 15 minutes. They should be starting to colour underneath before anything else joins them.

  4. Mix the glaze

    While the potatoes roast, whisk the soy sauce, honey, vinegar, ginger, garlic, sesame oil and chilli flakes in a small bowl until the honey has loosened. Grate the ginger on the fine side of a box grater so it melts into the sauce rather than sitting in threads.

  5. Add chicken and peppers

    Pull the tray out and push the potatoes to the edges. Lay the chicken in the middle, tuck the pepper strips around it, brush everything with the remaining oil and season. Roast for 18 minutes.

  6. Glaze in the last twelve minutes

    Spoon two thirds of the glaze over the chicken and vegetables, turning the peppers through it. Return to the oven for 12 minutes. The sauce should reduce to a syrup that clings to the chicken. If it looks like it is darkening too fast, drop the oven by 20°C.

  7. Check it is cooked

    The thickest part of a thigh should read 75°C on a probe thermometer, and the juices should run clear. Thigh is forgiving, so an extra three minutes will not ruin it.

  8. Rest, finish and serve

    Rest the tray for 5 minutes. Spoon over the remaining glaze, then scatter with spring onion, coriander leaves and toasted sesame seeds. Serve straight from the tray, with steamed rice if you want to stretch it further.

Cook's tips

  • Cut the potatoes to 3 cm. Anything larger will still be firm when the chicken is done. If your potatoes are big, quarter them rather than halving them, and keep the pieces even.
  • Do not line the tray with baking paper for this one. Paper insulates the base and stops the potatoes crisping, and the glaze sticks to it rather than reducing on the metal. Soak the tray in hot water while you eat instead.
  • Taste the glaze before it goes on. Soy sauces vary a lot in saltiness between brands. If yours tastes sharp, add another teaspoon of honey. If it tastes flat, add a splash more vinegar.
  • Give the tray room. If everything is touching, it steams. Use two trays if you are doubling the recipe, and swap their shelf positions halfway through.
  • In July and August, cook it in the evening. An oven at 220°C will heat a Cypriot flat for hours. Put the tray in after seven, eat late, and open the balcony door.

Storage

Cool leftovers within an hour, then refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Reheat in a 180°C oven, or 160°C fan-forced, for 12 to 15 minutes until piping hot through. The potatoes will not re-crisp in a microwave, but they are still good.

The cooked chicken freezes for up to 2 months. Freeze it without the potatoes, which go grainy. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Make ahead

The glaze keeps in a sealed jar in the fridge for 5 days, so it can be made on the weekend. Peppers and onion can be cut the night before and kept in a container. Do not salt the potatoes until they go in the oven, or they will weep.

Swaps and variations

  • Gluten free. Use tamari in place of soy sauce and check your rice wine vinegar is gluten free.
  • Sweet potato. Swap half the potatoes for sweet potato cut into 3 cm chunks, best in autumn when the local crop comes in, and add them 5 minutes later than the potatoes.
  • Vegetarian. Replace the chicken with 600 g of firm tofu, pressed and cut into thick slabs, and 400 g of tinned chickpeas, drained. Add both at the glaze stage.

Questions people ask about this recipe

You can, but change the timing. Add breast fillets 8 minutes later than the recipe says, so they roast for about 22 minutes in total, and pull them at 65°C rather than 75°C. Breast has far less fat, so it dries out quickly in a sticky glaze.

Almost always one of two things: the glaze went on too early, or the tray sat on the top shelf where the element is closest. Honey starts to catch after about fifteen minutes at this temperature. Keep the tray on the middle shelf and add the glaze at the twelve minute mark.

The chilli flakes are marked optional for exactly that reason. Leave them out entirely and the dish is sweet and savoury rather than hot. If you want heat for some of the table, mix the flakes into the reserved third of the glaze and only spoon that over the adult portions.

In two batches, yes. Cook the potatoes and onion at 200°C for 15 minutes, set them aside, then cook the chicken and peppers for 14 minutes, glaze, and give it another 5 minutes. Combine everything at the end. Most home air fryer baskets are too small to do it in one go without steaming.

Chicken, Ginger and Rice Noodle Soup

By Elena Christodoulou. Published 08/08/2026. Tested three times, twice with stock from a carton.

Not a pho, and not pretending to be. Cypriot kitchens have always taken in what arrives on the island and made it Tuesday dinner, and this bowl is that: bought stock, a lot of ginger, and a bare simmer, which is the only technique that really matters here. Forty minutes from cold pot to the table.

Never let it boil

A broth that boils turns cloudy and the chicken goes stringy. What you want is a bare simmer, where a bubble breaks the surface every second or so. On the bottled gas hobs in most Cypriot flats that is the smallest burner on its lowest setting, and on induction it is usually around setting three of nine.

Use thigh fillets, not breast. Thigh stays tender through twenty minutes of poaching and adds body to the broth. Breast is dry and papery by the time it is safely cooked.

The ginger quantity is not a typo. Eight centimetres of ginger, sliced into coins rather than grated, gives the broth its whole character without leaving fibres in the bowl. Buy a large knob, use half here, and keep the rest in the freezer where it grates more easily than fresh.

Where to shop for this one

Everything here is available in Cyprus, but not all in one shop. Alphamega and the larger Sklavenitis branches carry fish sauce, light soy, star anise and rice noodles in the international aisle. For anything beyond that, and for better prices, go to one of the Asian grocers: there are several around Agiou Andreou and the old port in Limassol, and a cluster near the Nicosia municipal market. That is where you will find flat pho noodles in more than one width, fresh buk choy, proper bean sprouts and fish sauce that is not four times the price.

If the greengrocer or the laiki has nothing Asian at all, spinach or the tender inner leaves of a Cypriot cabbage wilt into the broth perfectly well. This is a recipe for the shops you actually have.

Method

  1. Build the broth

    Put the stock, water, ginger coins, bruised garlic, onion halves and star anise in a large pot. Bring it up slowly and hold it at a bare simmer for 10 minutes so the aromatics open up.

  2. Poach the chicken

    Slide in the chicken thighs. Keep the heat at a bare simmer, never a boil, and poach for 18 minutes. Turn the thighs once. The broth should barely move.

  3. Rest and shred

    Lift the chicken onto a board and rest for 5 minutes, then shred it with two forks or slice it thickly. Resting first stops the juices running out into the board.

  4. Strain and season

    Strain the broth into a clean pot and discard the aromatics. Stir in the fish sauce, soy sauce and sugar. Taste it now. It should be savoury and slightly sweet, not salty. Adjust with a splash more water if your stock was strong.

  5. Soak the noodles

    Cover the rice noodles with boiling water in a heatproof bowl and stand for 6 minutes, until pliable but still firm. Drain and rinse briefly under cold water so they stop cooking, then divide between four deep bowls.

  6. Wilt the buk choy

    Drop the buk choy quarters into the hot broth for 2 minutes, just until the stems lose their squeak. Lift them out and sit them on top of the noodles.

  7. Assemble and serve

    Top each bowl with shredded chicken, ladle over the very hot broth, then finish with spring onion, coriander, chilli and bean sprouts. Serve the lime wedges on the side so everyone can sharpen their own bowl.

Cook's tips

  • The broth must be hotter than you think. It has to reheat cold noodles and raw sprouts on contact. Bring it back to just below boiling right before you ladle it out.
  • Do not cook the noodles in the broth. Rice noodles release starch that turns a clear broth cloudy and gluey. Soak them separately every time.
  • Save the coriander stems. Wash them and tie them into the broth with the ginger at step one, then strain them out. The stems carry more flavour than the leaves.
  • Freeze ginger. A knob of ginger keeps for months in the freezer and grates straight from frozen without stringing. Slice it into coins while frozen with a heavy knife.
  • Buy the fish sauce big. A large bottle from an Asian grocer costs less than a small supermarket one and keeps for a year in the cupboard. It quietly improves anything braised, including a Cypriot beef stew.

Storage

Store the broth, chicken and noodles in separate containers in the fridge for up to 3 days. Assembled soup does not keep, the noodles swell and break up. Reheat the broth to steaming and pour it over the cold components.

The broth alone freezes for 3 months and is worth making in a double batch for that reason.

Make ahead

Broth and chicken can be made a day ahead, which turns this into a fifteen minute dinner. Prepare the herbs and chilli just before serving so they stay crisp. Do not soak the noodles ahead.

Swaps and variations

  • Gluten free. Use tamari instead of soy sauce and check the fish sauce label. Rice noodles are naturally gluten free.
  • Vegetarian. Use a good vegetable stock, replace the fish sauce with 2 tablespoons of light soy plus 1 teaspoon of rice vinegar, and poach 400 g of firm tofu and a handful of dried shiitake in place of the chicken.
  • Bigger and richer. Add a soft boiled egg per bowl and a spoonful of chilli oil.
  • What is in the fridge. Winter spinach, wild greens or shredded cabbage all wilt into the broth in the buk choy step. Use lemon instead of lime if that is what the tree gave you.

Allergens

Contains fish (fish sauce), soy and gluten (soy sauce). Bought stock frequently contains gluten, celery and yeast extract, so read the carton. Always check the packaging on the products in your own kitchen and speak to a health professional about any dietary or medical need.
